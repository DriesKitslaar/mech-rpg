## N1: Overdrive

A meter the pilot fills by fighting; full, it buys one great move, once.

1. Drive: 0 to 100, on the pilot's mech only. Landing a volley adds 8, taking one adds 10, a blast
   taken adds 10, destroying an enemy adds 15. It carries through the battles of a contract.
2. Three Overdrives, one fitted at a time, chosen on the pilot's screen, opened by level:
   Second Wind (level 3: energy back, every weapon and action ready again, this turn),
   Iron Skin (level 6: until the next turn, half damage and no stagger),
   Deadeye (level 9: this turn every shot hits and does a quarter more).
3. Using it is free and does not end the turn. The meter empties.
4. Shown as a meter at the edge of the field; when full, a row at the top of the battle menu.
5. Only in the pilot's own mech: not in a lent mech, the simulator or the Gauntlet (N2 has a relic).
   The simulations and the balance tests fight without it, so the measured numbers stand.
6. Auto Turn uses it sensibly. A drill teaches it. Two medals.

## N2: Gauntlet 2.0

1. Two doors before every open floor: two different floors to choose from, one of them sometimes
   an elite floor (tougher, with an enemy that has a mark; the draft after it has four on offer).
2. Marks on enemies (armoured, overclocked, swift, shielded): also for later use elsewhere.
3. Relics: after each warden, one of two; they bend a rule for the rest of the run.
4. A boon can be taken five times at most, so depth is bounded by the enemy's growth.

## Changes made while building

- N1: R1 sets the Overdrive off (no row in the battle menu): the field names the button while the
  meter is full. The Overdrive goes with the pilot, like the perks: it is flown in the patrol's
  lent mech too; only the simulator and the Gauntlet are fought without. Measured over the whole
  campaign with the reference builds: about one Overdrive a battle, the first around round 4, and
  2 to 6 points more contracts won (Deadeye most). The gains stay as planned.
- N1: the browser tests have a sixth file in their second half, `e2e/moon2.spec.ts`.
- N2: the six relics are Redline Core (the frame gets Second Wind, the meter goes from floor to
  floor), Salvage Rig (the patch between floors is 60% instead of 30%), Quartermaster Key (one more
  on offer), Spare Core (once, a floor lost is stood before again in a whole mech; closing the game
  on a floor spends it too), Glass Cannon (+30% damage, armour 15% thinner) and Hazard Pay (the run
  pays half as much again). Elites are behind one door of about two open floors in five, from floor
  3 on; the mark goes to the dearest machine. Marks: armoured +40% armour, overclocked +25% damage,
  swift +15 evasion, shielded a barrier of a tenth of its armour raised anew every turn of its own.
  Measured with the automatic pilot (60 runs): elite floors are won about 70% of the time against
  89% for plain ones; the median depth stays 7 to 8. Overdrives set off in the Gauntlet count for
  the pilot's medals. The first door of a floor is the floor as it was before there were doors.
- N3: seven portraits drawn (the six wanted and the Guild's broker), low quality, $0.058 together;
  the ledger stands at $3.99 of $14. A notice shows the face of whoever it is for. The wingman's
  line is written small at the left foot of the field (a row higher while the Overdrive's label is
  there), at most 41 letters with the name; it waits while somebody is talking and is left off
  where a new pilot is being told the buttons. Only a hired wingman speaks: an ally the story
  sends keeps quiet.
- N4: Text Speed is Slow, Normal, Fast or Instant (0.6, 1 and 2.5 times each screen's own speed, or
  all at once); Screen Effects is Full or Calm (calm: the field does not shake, nothing flashes
  across it, the boss warning does not blink, a unit that is hit lights up only a little). Both are
  stored only when they are not the default. The jukebox is the database's fourth page: every song
  has a row, one not heard yet keeps its name; A plays or stops; leaving brings the hub's song back.
  What has been heard is kept in the save (`heard`), written with the next save or on the way out;
  with the music off nothing counts as heard. The briefing's view now says whether it is still
  writing (`typing`).
