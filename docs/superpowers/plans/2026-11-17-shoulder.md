# Over the Shoulder Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Change where a battle is looked at from. Until now it was a flat side view: the pilot's mech on the left, the enemies on the right, all on one line in front of a wall of scenery. From now on it is looked at over the mech's shoulder: the pilot's mech stands near, in the lower left, seen from behind; what it fights stands further off, up and to the right, facing it; between them lies a floor that runs into the distance; and the camera moves.

**Architecture:** The battle's rules, events, effects and timings stay what they are; what changes is where things stand, which drawing of the pilot's mech is used, which part of a place's picture is shown, and a camera over all of it. Every place's picture (310x270) already has a wide floor in its lower third that the panel hid: the field now shows a lower band of the picture, so that floor is what the machines stand on, and the camera can travel up and down the picture. The mech from behind is new art, made the way the mechs from the front were: one drawing per family in a shared pose, cut into the same five layers, so that parts of different families still fit on each other. Effects are placed by points the battle hands them (`Scene`), so they follow the new places by themselves.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, Canvas 2D, vitest with recording canvases, Playwright with a clock stepped by hand; the art pipeline in `scripts/art` (image requests through the proxy, `sharp`, a shared palette).

**Spec:** the user's message after the Finish was delivered: the battle's perspective is what they like least, and Golden Sun is the inspiration. Inspiration, not a copy: what is taken is the idea of the view (one's own side near and from behind, the other side far and facing, a ground with depth, a camera that moves); the game's own art, interface and effects stay its own. The standing instruction holds: decide the rest, build it, deliver an APK per phase, merge nothing unasked.

## Global Constraints

- Nothing about the rules, the numbers or the texts of the game changes. A saved game from before still loads.
- Timings that sounds and tests hang on keep their values (`PROJECTILE_MS`, `SHELL_MS`, `HIT_MS`, `DEATH_MS`, `MESSAGE_MS`, `TEXT_MS`, `INTRO_MS`, `WARNING_MS`).
- While nothing moves, every picture is drawn pixel for pixel on the 310x270 grid. Only while the camera moves is anything drawn at another size.
- A hurried game (`fast`) has the camera standing at home and draws no motion. Screen Effects: Calm has the camera standing at home too: no travel, no zoom, no shake.
- The three levels of light keep their meaning; nothing new is drawn at "none" that is soft.
- A canvas that cannot make a gradient is drawn on without error.
- Outside a battle the mech is seen from the front, as it was: the garage, the hub, the shop, the bands of an Overdrive and of a boss.
- Image spend: 3.99 of 14 dollars were spent before this round. This round may spend up to 3 dollars, at low quality only, every request through the ledger. The proxy's address and key are never written into the repository.
- Every new English text needs Dutch (none is planned).
- Work continues on branches stacked on the unmerged Finish (`feat/finish-review`); nothing is merged without the user's word.

## Review Focus

- A build mixed from parts of different families, seen from behind: every layer must meet its neighbours (no gap at the neck, the shoulders or the waist), with every pair of legs there is (tracks, four legs, a hover unit).
- Three enemies, a boss as large as a picture can be, a duel against a mech built from parts, and a machine that flies: none may stand off the field, behind the pilot's mech, or with its bars outside the screen.
- A shot from every hand and from the shoulder, from the pilot and at the pilot: it must leave the weapon that is drawn and land on the machine that is drawn.
- The camera at every moment it moves: no edge of the picture may show, nothing may be left behind (a light, a wreck, a number), and a press must never be lost or delayed by it.
- Calm, hurried, and each level of light: the field must be complete and still in all of them.

## Changes made while building

### S1

- **The spike** (before this plan was written): Ronin from behind came out right at the first request; the starter family at the second, once the request said "redraw the first image in another design" instead of "draw the second image from behind" (the first wording kept the design and lost the pose: the stretched arm hung down). Cutting into layers keeps every layer in its place, as from the front. A head layer can bring a piece of the backpack with it: the instruction for heads now names the backpack as something to erase, and every head is looked at.
- **What was asked for, and what it cost.** Eleven drawings from behind (eight families, three lower bodies) and forty-three layers cut from them; three heads (Ronin, Bulwark, Mantis) were cut a second time with words of their own (`HEAD_FROM_BEHIND`) because the first cut kept a piece of the pack. 1.19 dollars in all, at low quality; the ledger stands at 5.18 of 14.
- **A view is a row of a table.** `VIEWS` in `mechLayout.ts` holds what differs between the three views: the pose box, the suffix of the sprites, the two fists, the mount on the shoulder and the order of drawing. `layoutMech(sizes, view)` and `muzzleOf(sizes, slot, view)` read it; nothing else knows a view by name but `drawMech`, for the joints.
- **Weapons from behind carry their own grip and muzzle** (`gx`, `gy`, `mx`, `my` in the manifest), found when the sprite is made: turned and trimmed, a weapon's grip is no longer a fixed share of its box. From the front the shares stay what they were.
- **The head is drawn before the body from behind** (the plan had it after): a head cut from one drawing sat on top of the high back or the pack of a body cut from another. Beyond the body it tucks in behind whatever the body has there, as it would.
- **Three joints under the layers** (`BACK_JOINTS`: a neck, a bar across the shoulders, a waist; dark steel with a line of light), drawn by `drawMech` from behind only, where the body goes. Measured on the shipped pictures: without them 296 mixes of head, body and legs were in two pieces and 22 pairings of arm and body did not touch; with them, none. Where a family wears its own parts they are covered. From the front the same measure finds gaps too (they were always there and are not this round's to change).
- **Lower bodies that are no pair of legs sit lower from behind** (`LEGS_FROM_BEHIND` in `process.ts`: how far what stands on tracks, four legs or a hover unit is lowered, and how far the hover unit itself), found on proof sheets.
- **`tests/art/backView.test.ts` reads the shipped pictures:** every head on every body, every body on every pair of legs, every arm on every body make one figure; everything a pilot can carry has a grip that lies on it and a far end beyond and above the grip. `scripts/art/proof.ts back` (and `small`) writes the contact sheet of that view to `art/proof`.
- **Not done: a shield's inside.** A shield on the far arm shows its face from behind too. It is small and half hidden by the mech; left as it is.

### S2

- **A row for every place, not one for all** (the plan had one `HOME_ROW`). Where a picture's floor begins was read from each picture (`PLACES` in `stage.ts`: row 150 in the simulator, 223 at the sluice) and the band shown is chosen so that the floor begins 90 pixels down the field everywhere. Two pictures have their floor too low for that (the plaza just, the sluice by far): they are looked at from as low as a picture can be (`LOWEST_ROW`), and at the sluice the lines enemies stand on are closer together (`farLine`), so that nobody stands on the gates.
- **Three stand as a triangle with its point to the camera** (the plan's example had the third far right): the first two listed at the back, left and right, the third in front between them. What is listed first is as a rule the largest, and stands furthest. Over every line-up the story has, no machine's box is more than half behind those in front of it and every head is clear (`tests/ui/stage.test.ts`).
- **A machine too tall for its line is moved down, and then counts as nearer** (`standAt` returns the floor line under it; `farToNear` orders by that): an Overseer listed first is drawn after the tank listed second.
- **Bars that would lie on one another are stacked** (`placeBars`): two mechs and a gunship at the sluice have their feet within five pixels of each other, and their six bars were one smear. The higher stays, the lower is moved down until a pixel is between them; never under the panel.
- **The pilot's mech is drawn last**, after every enemy (it is the nearest thing there is), and the enemies' bars and marks after it (`drawEnemyMarks`, split off from `drawEnemies`).
- **The wingman's line and the new pilot's line moved to the top of the field** (`STRIP_Y`, under the strip that says what the battle asks for): the foot of the field is where the pilot's mech stands now.
- **What falls, falls to the floor under the machine it came off** (`floorUnder`): casings under the pilot's mech, wreckage of a far drone on the far line. Before, everything fell to one line at the foot of the field.
- **The lights of a place go with its picture, and its lowest part has lights of its own** (`Ambience.low`, drawn only where a `Lie` says the picture is looked at lower: a room shows that part under a panel). Shifted, the sluice kept one light of six and most places four: what glows in the newly seen part (lamps by the floor, what a wet floor mirrors) was found in the pictures and added; every place has five to eight on the field. Lights that have left the field are not drawn.
- **Haze lies along two lines**: where the floor begins, far off, and thinner at the pilot's feet. **Rain lands on the whole floor** (`drawWeatherExtras(..., floor)`), not on a strip at the foot of the field.
- **Where things are can be asked of the screen** (`motion.at`: where a weapon fires from, where a hit lands, a unit's middle, the floor under it), for the tests that check the Review Focus line about shots.
- **Left for later phases:** a lunge and a kick still move a machine left and right only (S4), and the camera stands at home (S3).

### S3

- **A view is the point of the stage in the middle of the field and how close it is** (`View { x, y, zoom }`; the plan had a row and an offset): one form for the way down the picture, a push and the close look at the last machine, which is the view `{ focus, 2 }`. `HOME` is one object, and `applyView(HOME)` does nothing at all: at home the stage is the screen, pixel for pixel, with no transform and no clip.
- **The way down** (`openingView`): from the top row of the place's picture to home over the first seven tenths of the banner's time, slow at both ends, by whole pixels at the picture's own size, so every frame of it is pixel for pixel too. A boss's warning lasts longer and so does the way down. A press sends the banner off and the field is at home in that very frame.
- **A push** (`Camera.push(focus, amount, delay)`): in over 90 ms, held 110, out over 340, on the field's own clock (held when the field is held, faster with battles played faster). A bullet or a beam that lands pushes in by 6 percent, a shell 9, a blade and a machine going up 12, an Overdrive going off 8 (`PUSH`); never closer than 15. It starts as the blow lands, not as it is fired; a miss moves nothing. The camera turns towards what was hit as far as the picture lets it: closer in, there is more room to turn. Of several at once the strongest has the picture. (Both of these were changed after the review: a push stays on the field, and pushes share the picture. See S5.)
- **Not done: a rise after a win and a sinking after a loss** (the plan had them). Rising would put the pilot's mech's feet under the panel, and the two places that are looked at from as low as a picture can be have no room to sink. The result's own light and loss of colour are what was there, and stay.
- **The close look has the picture to itself** (`Camera.settle()` as it begins): the push of the blow that ended the battle does not go on under it or come back after it.
- **What is in view** (`seenBy(view)`): the darker edges are the edges of that, a light of the place is drawn if it reaches into it (the signs high in a picture are seen while the camera is up there), the white of a held blow and lightning cover it, and while the camera is higher than the field it rains through the part above the field too. The haze and where rain lands stay on the stage.
- **Tests that look at the field look at it on the stage** (`onStage(drawn, screen.motion.view)` in `tests/helpers/canvas.ts`): a recording puts back everything that was drawn inside the field's clip to where and how large it is on the stage. Eleven tests that count lights or find an eye by its size were measuring a pushed-in field; they now measure the stage, and what the camera does is tested by itself.

### S4

- **A scene may say three more things** (`Scene.depth`, `heading`, `foot`, all optional): how far off a unit is, which way it looks at the other side, and where it stands on the floor. A scene that says none of them is flat, and the effects in it are what they were: life size, aimed between two points. The effects' own tests keep their flat scenes; the battle screen says all three.
- **How large for how far** (`sizeAt`): a fifth larger than life as near as the pilot's mech, seven tenths on the farthest line. A barrel's fire and its light are as large as the barrel is near; what flies has a size where it leaves and one where it lands (`grow`), so a shot from the pilot shrinks as it goes and one at the pilot grows; what strikes, breaks off or goes up is as large as where it does.
- **On the pixel grid "smaller" is thinner**: a tracer near the camera is two pixels thick with a head of three, far off its head is one; a shell's circle is drawn at a whole radius, two near and one far.
- **A beam narrows or widens** (`drawTaper` in `light.ts`: four four-cornered bands instead of four strokes). In a flat scene it is stroked at one width as before.
- **Two directions, not one.** What is fired goes from the barrel to what it is aimed at (`aim`): its fire points that way and its mech is kicked back along it. What moves over the floor (a blade's lunge, a boost, a bomb that throws itself) goes from where the one stands towards where the other does (`foot`), which on the screen is up the floor for the pilot and down it for an enemy: from body to body the line is all but level, and a lunge along that did not look like going away from the camera. A guard stands across the way its unit looks.
- **The whole machine moves** (`UnitTransform.lift`, apart from `dy`, which throws only what stands on the legs): the pilot's mech lunges legs and all. Its after-images stand behind it on its line (`trailY`).
- **A miss flies on along its line and wide on the upper side** (it used to end at a fixed place up and to the right of the target).
- **The generator's light** (`GENERATOR`, `drawGenerator`): a small warm light on the back of the pilot's mech, over its body, that breathes, grows and brightens with the mech's heat, sinks to a glimmer while the mech is dazed, and goes where the body goes. Like an eye it is not one of the battle's sixteen lights; a hurried game has none; a calm picture has it at half strength.
- **Not done:** the kick of a gun in the layers' own poses (`pose.ts`) still goes left and right; with three pixels of whole-mech kick along the barrel on top of it, it reads as it should.

### S5

- **A list of small deliberate faults, one at a time, in the round's new code**: 226 of them, in `stage.ts`, `camera.ts`, `mechLayout.ts`, `mechView.ts`, `battleFx.ts`, `fxPaint.ts`, `light.ts`, `ambience.ts`, `weather.ts` and the battle screen. 212 were found by the tests as they stood. Of the 14 that were not, one showed a guard that could never fire (a hurried game was told not to move its camera, but nothing is staged there that would ask it to): removed. The other 13 each got a test and are found now: bars that would only touch, the point a push was told with, the line the sky's light is laid along, the shaken picture's place, a bomb going off moving the camera, the generator's light on a lunging mech, a mech fought alone kicked the right way though it is drawn turned round, what the pilot's mech looks at, paint from behind and small, an ally's fire, a steep lunge's after-images, and the far end of a lunge's jet.
- **Writing the list found things before any fault was run**, each with its test: the cap on the farthest line and the two clamps of a duel's box had nothing that reached them; the flyers were named nowhere but in the code; the camera's turn was checked for smoothness across but not up and down; where rain lands, where debris falls and how hard a shell and a blade push were checked nowhere on the battle screen itself.
- **The generator's light went out too soon**: when the rules had the mech destroyed, which is before the shot that ends it has landed. It goes out in the frame the mech is seen to come apart, as an eye does.
- **A held blow holds the camera too**, so the white of it and a push meet only when one blow lands on the heels of another (battles played faster). The white is laid over the whole field, which covers what a pushed-in camera has in view; the test brings the two together by hand.
- **The soak** (`SOAK=1 SOAK_MINUTES=5`, two at a time): five players, 264,148 frames, 86 battles, four floors down the Gauntlet for three of them, every room, an ending and the credits; nothing a canvas refused, nothing caught by the game's guard, nothing on the console. A first run lost two of its five players for a reason that was not the game's: a source file was saved while they played, and the development server reloads the page when that happens. The soak is run with nothing else touched.
- **What a frame of a battle costs to draw, measured** in a browser without a graphics chip at the handheld's 1240x1080, every frame painted before it is timed. At rest: 5.6 to 9 milliseconds (a frame has 16.7). The camera costs about one more while it is pushed in. But while volleys land, one frame in twenty took 60 to 85 milliseconds, with the camera or without it: the cost was the canvas filter a unit was lit with when it was hit or fired (`ctx.filter = 'brightness(...)'`, there since the round that gave the field its light). A unit is now lit by laying its own picture over itself as light, once for each whole time brighter and once more for what is left (`BRIGHT` in the battle screen: 2.6 when hit, 1.4 in a calm picture, 1.35 by its own gun): the same brightness, no filter. After: 6 to 8 milliseconds through volleys, one frame in twenty at 10.
- **A mech drawn from its parts is laid over itself whole.** The first version of the above laid each layer over itself, and looking at it closely afterwards showed what that does where layers lie over each other: the one underneath adds its light through the one on top. In one pixel in seven of the pilot's mech the body shone through an arm or a weapon, by up to 116 levels of 255 by its own gun and up to 253 when hit. The mech is now drawn whole on a sheet of its own first (`layMech` in `mechView.ts`; the sheet is one small canvas the sprite store lends, `SpriteStore.sheet`), and the sheet is laid on: every layer of a mech sits on whole pixels, so the sheet lies on the mech exactly. Compared with the filter in a browser, whole frames of a real battle, pixel by pixel: within one level of 255 everywhere at rest, at four and at three times the game's size, for the pilot's mech (hit, firing, calm, thrown aside), a mech fought alone (drawn turned round) and a machine that is one picture; with the camera pushed in, under fifty pixels on edges differ. A machine that is one picture needs no sheet. A store that has no sheet to lend (the tests' own, or a browser that refuses one) gets the layers one by one as before. The cost is the same as it was: 6.5 to 8 milliseconds through volleys, one frame in twenty at 9 to 12. The game is only ever drawn at a whole number of times its size (`computeScale`); at three and a half the sheet would lie a pixel off.
- **48 more small faults, in the sheet and how it is used**: 45 found by the tests written with it. One was not, and got a test: the light on a mech that is going down has to sink with it. The other two change nothing that can be seen: a browser that gives a canvas but nothing to draw on it with is caught three lines further on either way.
- **The Calm option's help line** says what Calm now also means: "Calm: no shaking, no flashes, a still camera." (Dutch: "Rustig: geen schudden of flitsen, camera stil.")

### S5: the independent review

Someone who had not built any of it was given the branch as it stood and no account of what it was meant to be beyond the plan, and came back with eleven defects, 71 small faults of 106 that no test caught, fourteen tests that proved less than they said, and eleven places where less code would be safer. Each is answered here. Nothing was merged.

**Defects**

- **Paint of the front view (D1).** Making the views from behind had shifted how what was already there takes paint: 21 entries of the front view (Bulwark, Mantis, Colossus, Auditor, and the tracks), and with them how a painted Bulwark or Mantis looks in the garage, by up to 61 levels of 255 in a sixth of its pixels. The small copies kept the front layers' references and were counted among what a family is judged by. A family is judged by what was cut from its own drawing from the front (`paintGroups` in `jobs.ts`); its views from behind, its small copies and legs edited onto it take its paint and do not shift it. The 172 entries that were there before the round are what they were, to the letter. A test works the paint out again from each family's shipped pictures and holds the manifest to it: it fails on the manifest as it was.
- **Lamps at less light (D2).** Where there is to be less light half of a place's lamps are lit. Which half was chosen from what the camera had in view, so lamps went out and came on as it moved. It is settled by the field as it stands: half of those the field shows, and half of the others (`drawAmbience`). The picture at home is what it was; a camera that moves only brings lamps into view and takes them out of it.
- **The generator's light (D3)** was fixed before the review came back (above).
- **The floor slid under the feet at every jolt (D4).** The thrown picture was drawn twelve pixels larger about its own middle. That middle used to be the middle of the screen; the field now shows the picture's lower band, far from it, and the stretch moved the floor up to six pixels under feet that were thrown one to five. The picture goes where the field goes, as it is, pixel for pixel, and its outermost rows and columns are drawn out beyond it so that no edge shows (`EDGE`, one pixel more than `HARDEST_JOLT`, the hardest blow there is; a jolt asked for harder than that is that hard).
- **The camera (D5, D6).** Two changes to what S3 built. A push shows a part of the field as it stands and nothing from the rows a picture has to spare over and under it (`inField`, which replaces `inPicture`): the camera turns as far up and down as it does across, and no further. It used to look down a picture as far as the picture went, 25 pixels in a tenth of a second at a low target, and then there was no rain in the strip it showed under the field: that strip is not looked at any more. And of several pushes at once the closest says how close the camera is, and each has its say in where it looks by how far in it is, one that is only beginning having little yet. It used to be the strongest outright, and the picture jumped 18 pixels in a frame when another took over. Measured, frame to frame: a push by itself moves the picture at most 7.2 pixels across and 3.8 up or down (the closest there is, in its first frame); two of the hardest a battle has, from opposite corners of the field and at any moment, 7.5 and 4.0.
- **A result (D7).** Pushes went on under a result that was pressed up early. What ends when a result comes up ends in one place (`endStaging`), after every press and every step of time: the band of an Overdrive and every push, going on or still to come. - A mech that is fought alone holds its left-hand weapon behind its legs too, as every mech from the front always did. It is small and far off, and it was not changed.

**Faults no test caught**

- The reviewer's 71 were run again against the tests as they came to stand. Every one is found by a test now, or was a fault in code that has been taken out (the cap on the farthest line, two clamps on a duel's box, a check the radio could never fail, a second check on a result).
- The tests that did most of it: two battles played alike, press for press and frame for frame, one of them through a camera that never leaves home, and every frame of the other, put back on the stage, is the frame of the first (the opening, every blow, the Overdrive, the close look). That shows anything the camera moves wrongly. It does not show a thing the camera leaves behind, which is drawn where the stage has it either way: so what stands or happens on the field is named (bars, what flies off machines, smoke, the Overdrive's rings, what is fired, figures, every light) and each is seen to be drawn inside the field while the camera is away.
- What is painted along a line is tested at four slopes, steep ones among them, where it was tested level. What flies is tested for the size of each part of it.
- 96 more faults, in what was written in answer to the review and in the reviewer's own put again to code that had been rewritten since. Three are left that nothing finds. Two were in code that did nothing (the close look ended a second time; a mark kept clear of its own machine's bars, which are under its feet) and that code is gone. The third is a step of the art pipeline, which no test runs: what the pipeline writes is held to the rule by the test on the shipped pictures, so a wrong step shows the next time pictures are made.

- The shaken picture, the push of two at once, hits on lowered legs, a duelling mech's muzzle (the exact far end of what it holds, on its picture), the real sprite store (`spriteOf`, with loading itself), what a family's paint is judged by, a mech in all three views: each is tested now for what its name says.

- Loose bounds made tight: the generator's calm light, how much smaller what is carried is from behind.
