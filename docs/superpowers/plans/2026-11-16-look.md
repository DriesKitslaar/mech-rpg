# The Look Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Make IRONWAGE look impressive on the handheld's 1240x1080 screen: light, weight and motion in battle, a field that is never still, an interface with a face of its own, a title that announces itself, and a few moments staged like cinema.

**Architecture:** The sprites and backgrounds stay as they are, pixel for pixel. Everything new is drawn by the game at run time, in two registers: what is solid (debris, fire, smoke, frames, icons) stays on the 310x270 pixel grid, and what is light (glow, shadow, haze, beams) is drawn soft at the screen's own resolution and added on top. All of it follows from a clock and the events of the battle, as the existing effects do, so it needs no stored state and can be stepped frame by frame.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, Canvas 2D (`globalCompositeOperation: 'lighter'`, radial gradients), vitest with stand-in canvases, Playwright with a clock that is stepped by hand.

**Spec:** the user's one line, "now it has to look visually impressive", with the standing instruction to decide the rest. The decisions are in this document.

## Global Constraints

- Nothing about the rules, the numbers, the saves or the texts changes. Timings that sounds and tests hang on (`PROJECTILE_MS`, `SHELL_MS`, `HIT_MS`, `DEATH_MS`, `TEXT_MS`) keep their values.
- A game that is hurried (`fast`, which is how the tests and most of Playwright run) draws none of the motion: it must stay as quick and as still as it is.
- The option Screen Effects: Calm keeps its promise ("no shaking and no flashes in battle") for everything new: no light that covers the whole field, no lightning, no zoom, no shake; local glow at half strength.
- A canvas that cannot make a gradient (the tests' stand-ins) is drawn on without error: every soft-light helper is a no-op there.
- Text stays where it is: no label, figure or hint moves, so the 310x270 layouts the tests pin still hold.
- The handheld must keep sixty frames a second: at most 16 lights and 320 particles alive at once, no per-pixel work (`getImageData`) in a frame.
- New art only where code cannot do it, low quality, and the whole round within 3 dollars (ledger now at 3.99 of 14).
- Dutch for every new text (there should be next to none).
- Work continues on branches stacked on `feat/moon2-review3` (PR 34); nothing is merged without the user's word.

## Review Focus

- Calm mode with every new effect on screen at once (an explosion, a beam, a death, a cut-in): nothing covers the field, nothing shakes.
- A battle played at Fast Battles speed and in Auto Turn: effects overlap and pile up; the light and particle ceilings hold and nothing lingers after the result.
- The four enemies standing closest together with bars, marks and tags: the new shadows, glows and frames must not hide a bar or a word.
- Dutch, the longest labels, in every skinned panel: the frame's corners and tabs must not cut into text.
- A canvas at a scale that is not four (a phone at 3x, a browser at 2x or 5x): soft light and frames must still line up with the pixel grid.

## Approaches considered

1. **Redraw the art at a higher resolution.** The most obvious, and the worst: the sprites are the game's best feature, a paper-doll of layers that fit each other, and a second set would take hundreds of images that would no longer match.
2. **Stay strictly on the pixel grid** (dithered glow, squares for smoke). Authentic, but it is what the game does today, and it is why its effects read as dots.
3. **Pixel sprites under soft light** (chosen). Solid things stay pixels; light, shadow and haze are smooth and additive. It is what makes modern pixel games read as rich on a dense screen, it costs no art, and it can be stepped and photographed frame by frame, which is the only way this can be judged without a person watching.

## L1: The stage, light, and battle effects

What it is: a way to photograph any moment of the game, a small module for soft light, and every attack, hit and death redone with it.

**Files:**
- Modify `src/main.ts`: with `?step=1` the frame loop is not started; `window.__game.advance(ms: number)` updates and draws once per call. Nothing else changes.
- Create `src/ui/light.ts`: `Glow { x, y, r, color, alpha }`, `drawGlow(ctx, glow, strength = 1)`, `drawGlows(ctx, glows, strength)` (at most `MAX_LIGHTS = 16`, the brightest kept), `drawShadow(ctx, x, y, w, h, alpha)`, `drawBeam(ctx, from, to, width, color, alpha)`, `disc(ctx, x, y, r, color)` (a filled circle on the pixel grid), `ringOf(ctx, x, y, r, color)` (its outline), `FIRE: string[]` (white to smoke, seven steps) with `fireColor(p: number): string`.
- Modify `src/ui/battleFx.ts`: new timed kinds `flash`, `fireball`, `shock`, `trail`; `lights(): Glow[]` from what is active; projectiles, bursts, slashes, shields, gathers and steam redrawn; a unit destroyed goes in a chain of small blasts and one large.
- Modify `src/ui/particles.ts`: `MAX_PARTICLES = 320`; kinds `fire` and `puff`; a particle may `glow`; `lights(): Glow[]` for those that do (the brightest few).
- Modify `src/ui/battleScreen.ts`: shadows under every unit, lights drawn over the field (`calm`: half strength, never the whole field), a unit that fires or is struck is relit for a moment, a wreck keeps smoking.
- Tests: `tests/ui/light.test.ts`, `tests/ui/battleFx2.test.ts`, more in `tests/ui/particles.test.ts` and `tests/ui/motion.test.ts`; `tests/helpers/canvas.ts` records gradients, arcs and the blend mode.
- Frames: `e2e/look.spec.ts` (stepped clock) writes `docs/screenshots/look-*.png`.

**Tests that pin it:** a glow on a stand-in canvas draws nothing and throws nothing; on a recording canvas it is one radial gradient, additive, and the blend mode is put back; `drawGlows` keeps the 16 brightest; `fireColor(0)` is white and `fireColor(1)` the smoke; each damage type schedules its own kinds at the old times (`kinds()` at 0, flight and flight + hit); `lights()` is empty when nothing is active and never more than 16; a hurried screen has no lights, shadows excepted; calm halves their strength and draws no field-wide flash; after the result nothing is left alive.

## Changes made while building

- L1 as built: the painting of effects is its own file (`src/ui/fxPaint.ts`: `paintFx` for what is solid,
  `lightsOf` for the light it throws), so `battleFx.ts` is left with what happens when. Particles got one
  new kind, `fire`, not two: nothing needed a `puff`. Added along the way, because the frames asked for
  them: the one that fires is lit by its own gun for a moment (`glare`), a unit that lunges burns a jet
  behind it and leaves two after-images (`trail`), figures have a dark edge all round so that they read
  over fire, a machine that went up keeps smoking and smouldering where it stood. A barrel's flash, a
  beam and a blast were looked at frame by frame (`LOOK_FRAMES=<folder>` with `e2e/look.spec.ts` writes
  every frame of three turns); the boost and the vent were too thin on the first look and were redone.
  In a calm picture a machine's last blast is drawn without its light: that light is wider than the
  field light a calm picture allows.
- L2 as built: where a place's lights are was not chosen by eye but found: each background was
  searched for its brightest coloured spots (the signs, lamps and molten iron that are drawn there),
  and the table in `src/ui/ambience.ts` is what the search found, with the arena's floodlights added
  by hand. Each place has a kind (`neon` hums and drops out, `lamp` breathes, `fire` flickers,
  `steady` swells) and most have a haze. Dropped from the plan: a colour grade and light shafts
  (the lights and the haze already did what they were for), and sensors that glint (it would need
  to know where each machine's eye is). Added: the edges of the field are darker than its middle;
  what flies burns underneath; a mech close to its heat limit glows and the air over it wavers.
  Lightning was first a wash of light added over the field, which turned the picture milky; it now
  lights the field in a way that leaves dark things dark, and whitens the sky from the top down.
  A hurried game does not leave the place out, as the plan said, but has it standing still: the
  pictures the tests take then look like the game.
- L3 as built: every screen draws its boxes with `drawPanel`, so the one function gave all of them
  the new face: a plate that is lighter at the top, corners cut by two pixels, a frame that steps
  round them, a line of light under the top edge, the game's colour at the top left and the bottom
  right, and a soft shadow under the box. A panel sixteen pixels high or lower is a bar across the
  screen, and only the one at the top of the screen carries the colour at its left end (the shop's
  strip of slots under it does not: the mark sat under the first slot's name like a second open
  page). Bars got depth where they have the height for it, and marks at every tenth where they have
  the length. The row the cursor is on is lit from the left; four screens draw their own rows (the
  garage's slots and its saved builds, the weapons in battle, the medals) and were given the same
  light. Dropped: icons (they would have moved every label they stood beside) and the pale trail
  on a bar where armour was just lost (a bar has no memory of what it showed before, and the
  figures that fly up already say it). Found while looking at the pictures, and older than this
  round: in a new pilot's hub the locked Gauntlet's row had its name and its note touching. A note
  that would run into the name beside it is now written smaller.
- L4 as built: the name on the title is no longer written in the interface's font. It is a sign
  (`src/ui/logo.ts`): each letter is the path a broad pen with cut corners is drawn along, worked
  out once into rows of runs, with three pixels of depth, a dark line round it and a face like
  polished metal, IRON in steel and WAGE in the game's colour. It comes on like a tube (dark, a
  strike, dark, a second that falters, on), holds but for a moment's dip now and then, throws its
  own light, and a leaning gleam crosses it every few seconds. Where the platform has a canvas to
  keep it on it is painted once, lit and unlit, and drawn from there. The picture under it is no
  longer veiled: its lights were found in it as the places' were (`src/ui/titleScene.ts`: the
  mine's lamps, the windows, three beacons that come and go on the stack, the eye of the machine on
  the roof), mist drifts over the water, two searchlights lean about and cross the sign, and the
  rain now lands and brings lightning. The menu moved to the lower left: in the middle it stood on
  the machine the picture is about. This is the one place in the round where text moved.
  Rooms (`src/ui/room.ts`): the hangar behind the hub and the garage, the vault, the arena and the
  simulator are lit by their own lights behind the mech and again, weaker, over it, with a shadow
  at its feet; in the hangar somebody welds up on the walkway. Between screens (`src/ui/wipe.ts`):
  a dark blind with a lit, leaning edge is drawn off a new screen, and the doors over a battle have
  a lit edge and a seam. Dropped: the swinging hook (it is part of the picture), and the tall
  picture for the title to pan down (no new art was needed: the round has spent nothing).
  The limit of sixteen lights is kept for each thing that is lit: the picture has at most seventeen
  small ones of which some beacon is always dark, and the sign adds six of its own.
