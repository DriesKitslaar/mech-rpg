# The Finish Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Close what the round of the look left open: make sure the handheld keeps its pace whatever the new light costs, give what is now seen something to be heard by, write the game's big words in the letters of its own sign, bring the story's pages to life the way the title was, and give bosses an entrance and machines an eye.

**Architecture:** As in the look: what is solid stays on the 310x270 pixel grid, what is light is soft and added, and everything follows from a clock and the events of the game. New here is one piece of state that is not the game's: how well the handheld is keeping up, measured from the time between frames, which sets how much soft light is drawn.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, Canvas 2D, Web Audio (sounds built from tones, as all of them are), vitest with recording canvases, Playwright with a clock stepped by hand.

**Spec:** the user's question after the look was delivered, "do you still see points for improvement?", with the standing instruction to decide the rest and get on with it. The five points this plan answers with are in the Goal.

## Global Constraints

- Nothing about the rules, the numbers or the texts of the game changes. A saved game from before still loads; the settings gain one field, and a file without it means what the game did before.
- Timings that sounds and tests hang on keep their values (`PROJECTILE_MS`, `SHELL_MS`, `HIT_MS`, `DEATH_MS`, `MESSAGE_MS`, `TEXT_MS`, `INTRO_MS`, `WARNING_MS`).
- A hurried game (`fast`) draws no motion and plays no new sound that hangs on motion.
- Screen Effects: Calm keeps every promise it has: no shaking, no flashes, no lightning, no zoom, soft light at half strength, no light over the whole field. No thunder where there is no lightning.
- A canvas that cannot make a gradient is drawn on without error.
- The big words keep their place: where a word is written in the sign's letters instead of the interface's font, its middle is where it was, and it never leaves its box or the screen in either language. A word the sign cannot write is written in the font it was in.
- Every new English text needs Dutch.
- No new art is planned: the ledger stays at 3.99 of 14 dollars.
- Work continues on branches stacked on `main`; nothing is merged without the user's word.

## Review Focus

- A handheld that is fast but stumbles once (an app switch, a pause) must not have its light turned down; one that is slow must have it turned down within seconds; and it must never go back and forth.
- Thunder on a title left open for an hour, and in a long battle in the rain: never more than one at a time, never in calm, never with sound off.
- The sign's letters with the longest Dutch words, with digits, and with a character it has no shape for.
- A page of story with the most text there is, in both languages, over the brightest of the pictures: every line must read.
- A boss's entrance pressed away at once, in a hurried game, in calm, and for a boss that is a mech built from parts as well as one that is a single picture.

## F1: Keeping pace

Nobody here has the handheld. The look's light costs fill: two to six screens of gradient a frame. If its graphics chip takes that in its stride nothing here is ever seen; if not, the game must notice and give light up before it gives smoothness up.

**Files:**
- Create `src/ui/pace.ts`: `Pace` (fed the time between two frames; `level: Detail`), `Detail = 2 | 1 | 0` (full, lean, bare), `WINDOW = 90` frames, `SLOW_MS = 19.5` (the middle frame of a window takes longer than this: the handheld is not keeping up), `STEADY_WINDOWS = 2` (so many slow windows in a row before a step down), `PAUSE_MS = 250` (a longer gap is a pause, not a slow frame: it is left out and the window starts again), no step back up within a session.
- Modify `src/ui/light.ts`: `setDetail(level)`, `detail()`. At 0 every soft-light helper draws nothing, as on a canvas without gradients. At 1 `MAX_LIGHTS` counts as 8, and what lies over a whole field or screen (edges, haze, washes, cones, shadows under boxes) is left out.
- Modify `src/ui/app.ts`, `src/main.ts`: the app is told each frame's gap, keeps a `Pace`, and sets the detail from the setting: `auto` follows the pace, the others hold.
- Modify `src/core/game.ts` (settings), `src/ui/optionsScreen.ts`, `src/content/strings.ts`, `src/content/nl/`: one option, Light Effects: Auto, Full, Reduced, Off, with a line of help.
- Modify `src/ui/light.ts`: the rows of a circle are worked out once for each radius.
- Modify `src/ui/inputDebug.ts`: the frames a second and the detail level, where the screen's size is shown.
- Tests: `tests/ui/pace.test.ts`, more in `tests/ui/light.test.ts`, the options tests and the Dutch tests.

**Tests that pin it:** sixty frames at 16.7 ms hold full; two windows at 25 ms step down once, two more step down again, and nothing ever steps up; one frame of two seconds among fast ones changes nothing; a window that is half slow, half fast is judged by its middle frame; at lean only eight lights are drawn and no haze, edge or cone; at bare nothing soft is drawn and nothing fails; the setting `full` holds full on a slow handheld; a settings file without the field is `auto`.

## Changes made while building

### F1

- **A window is judged by its share of late frames, not by its middle frame.** The plan had `SLOW_MS = 19.5` for the middle frame of a window and `STEADY_WINDOWS = 2`. On a screen that shows sixty frames a second a frame takes one sixtieth or two, never something in between, so the middle frame says only whether more than half were late. What was built: a frame is late from `LATE_MS = 25`, a window of `WINDOW = 90` frames is slow when more than `LATE_SHARE = 0.2` of them are late, and `SLOW_WINDOWS = 3` slow windows in a row give a step up. The first `WARM_UP = 120` frames of a session are not judged.
- **Giving light up has to help.** Not in the plan, and the reason the monitor is more than a counter. A handheld can be slow for reasons that have nothing to do with light: a screen or a power setting that shows thirty frames a second. The plan's monitor would have taken all the light from such a handheld and gained nothing. Now, when everything has been given up in one go and the frames are no better for it (late frames not a quarter rarer, `HELPED = 0.75`; frames not fifteen in a hundred shorter, `SHORTER = 0.85`), the light goes back to where the coming down began, once, and nothing is judged any more that session. A single window in which the handheld kept up ends the coming down: what was given up then stays given up.
- **What was found is kept from one session to the next** (`PACE_KEY`, `PaceHint { level, runs, held? }`, apart from games and options: it is a fact about the handheld). Without this every session on a slow handheld would start with seconds of stutter before the light went down. A session starts where the last one ended; after `TRY_AGAIN = 5` sessions it starts a step higher and is judged again. `held` marks a handheld whose light was found not to be what it is slow at: it is left alone for as many sessions.
- **While the player's own choice holds, nothing is found out and nothing kept is touched.** Handed back to Auto, the game forgets what it knew and judges afresh from all of the light.
- **Three levels, and what each leaves out.** Less (`lean`): half of every set of lights, the brightest (a battle's sixteen become eight), and nothing that lies over a whole field or screen: darker edges, haze, cones of light, the wash of a won field, the shadow under boxes, the lit sky of a stroke of lightning, and the pass that takes the colour out of a lost field (the field is darker instead). None (`bare`): nothing soft at all, which is the game as it has always been drawn on a canvas without gradients, so nothing new can fail there; lightning is left out with it.
- **The debug window** (the menu button) shows `pace: 60 fps, light 2`: the one way to learn from a distance what the handheld does.
- The tests of the levels are in `tests/ui/detail.test.ts` (not in `light.test.ts`): they cross every module that draws soft light.
- **Mutations**: 106 deliberate faults in the new code. 101 were found by the tests as they stood; five were not (a frame of exactly 25 ms; gaps of zero mixed in with real frames; the room's spill at less light; the pace kept when handed back to Auto; every change of the option judged afresh). Each now has a test, and all 106 are found.

### F2

- **No sound of its own for the Overdrive's band.** The band comes up in the frame the Overdrive's own sound begins, and that sound opens with a rush of noise: a second one on the same frame would only make it louder. Three sounds were added, not four: `thunder`, `sign`, `slam`.
- **The slam takes the place of the machine's first boom; it does not join it.** The blow that ends a battle is heard as the slam while the field is held; the machine is heard to go up when it is seen to, in the close look. A press that ends the look early leaves the slam as all that was heard: no sound is put by for later.
- **The music is turned down by a state, not by a timer.** The plan had `duck(level, ms)`. What was built is `Music.hush`, a share the app sets every frame from what is on screen, as it sets `tense`: whatever ends the close look (its own end, a press, the result) ends the hush with it. `HUSH = 0.35`, down in `HUSH_IN = 0.05` seconds, back in `HUSH_OUT = 0.3`, never below `HUSH_FLOOR = 0.1`.
- **Tones gained two optional things**: `cut`, a ceiling in hertz above which everything is taken off, and `fade`, the loudness a tone ends on. Thunder cannot be made from bare noise that dies away at once. Its roll is five bursts of noise with their tops off, each held until the next has taken over. An engine that makes no filters plays the tones with their tops on.
- **Sounds nobody pressed for are `ambient`** (third argument of `Sfx.play`): an engine that is asleep, as a browser's is before its first input, does not keep them. Without this every clap of thunder since the page was opened would be heard together at the first press.
- `thunderDue` became `strokeBegan(before, now, calm)`; the sign's strikes are `SIGN_STRIKES` and `signStrikes`. Thunder is asked for `THUNDER_AFTER_MS = 380` after its stroke begins: it is some way off.
- **Measured, since nobody here can listen**: rendered offline, thunder peaks at 0.26 of full scale and is heard for two seconds over the title's song (0.03 rms rising to 0.07 at the clap); the slam peaks at 0.44 with the music at a third of itself; nothing clips.
- The tests are in `tests/ui/heard.test.ts`, with the engine's in `tests/platform/audio.test.ts` and `tests/platform/music.test.ts`.
- **Mutations**: 59 deliberate faults in the new code; 55 were found by the tests as they stood. Of the other four, one showed a guard that an outer one already covers (taken out: an engine that cannot move its loudness is asked once and plays on), one needed a test (a move of the loudness starts from where it is at that moment), and two change nothing that can be told: whether the loudness is settled before or after a frame's notes are queued, and the number the moment of a stroke is drawn from, which lightning and thunder share by being one function.
