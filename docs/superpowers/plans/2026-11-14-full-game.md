## Global constraints

Unchanged from plans 1 to 4, plus:

- **Image budget.** `scripts/art/generate.ts` writes every request to `art/ledger.json` (id, date,
  quality, method, token usage when the proxy returns it, estimated USD). It refuses to run when
  the ledger total would pass `ART_BUDGET_USD = 12`. Low quality only. Only the two image
  functions of the proxy are ever called. The proxy address and key never enter the repository.
- **Saves.** Each phase may raise the save version once. Every older version upgrades.
- **Process.** Failing test first for every rule. `timeout 250 npx vitest run`, `npx tsc --noEmit`,
  and the e2e suite must pass before a phase is pushed. One independent review (subagent, model
  `fable`, read-only) and one fix pass per phase. Draft PR per phase, stacked on the previous one.
  The APK of each phase is sent to the owner.
- **Never stuck.** The patrol stays. The last weapon cannot be sold. A build the garage accepts
  can always take a contract.
- **Text.** All player-facing text in `src/content` (strings.ts or the content file it belongs to).
  Original names only.

## Phase 1: Combat 2.0

### 1.1 New part kinds and slots

`Build` gains `booster`, `fcs`, `module`. Players must fit a booster and an FCS; a module is
optional. Enemies may leave all three out and then use the defaults below.

| Kind | Fields |
|---|---|
| `booster` | `thrust` (evasion while boosted), `cost` (fraction of turn energy to boost), weight, drain |
| `fcs` | `aim: Record<DamageType, number>`, `focus` (0..1: share of the limb penalty removed), weight, drain |
| `module` | `effect`, `power`, `uses` (per battle), weight, drain |
| `shield` | fits the left hand only; `guard` (damage multiplier while guarding), weight |

Defaults without a booster: thrust 30, cost 0.30 (today's numbers). Without an FCS: no bonus.

`FramePart.legType?: 'biped' | 'reverse' | 'quad' | 'tank' | 'hover'` (legs only, default biped):

| Type | Effect |
|---|---|
| reverse | +8 thrust |
| quad | stability ×1.25, shoulder weapons +6 aim |
| tank | stability ×1.4, cannot boost, guard multiplier 0.5 |
| hover | boost cost ×0.6, stability ×0.8 |

`Weapon` gains `impact?` (overrides the derived value), `charge?`, `melee?`, and the traits
`burst` (fires `traitPower` shots) and `salvo` (one shot at every living enemy).

Stock parts (weight 0, drain 0, so an upgraded save never becomes overweight):
`bst-stock` Stock Thrusters (30, 0.30, 100 cr), `fcs-stock` Stock FCS (no bonus, 100 cr),

### 1.2 Stability and stagger

- `staggerMax = round((60 + 0.07 × build weight) × legType factor × mods.toughness)`.
- A hit adds `impact`: the weapon's `impact`, else `damage × factor` (kinetic 1.0, explosive 1.4,
  energy 0.5). Halved against a guarding target. Each shot of a burst or twin counts.
- When the bar is full the unit is **staggered**: `staggered = 2`, charges are lost.
  While staggered: evasion 0, damage taken ×1.3, no boost, bar frozen.
- At the start of each of its own turns (skipped turns count): if staggered, `staggered -= 1`;
  at 0 the bar resets to 0. A turn begun with `staggered = 1` gets energy ×0.6.
  If not staggered, the bar drains by 35% of its maximum.
- So a stagger lasts through one whole turn of the attacker: stagger, the target's weak turn,
  one full turn to punish, recovery.

### 1.3 Guard

`guard`: costs `ceil(0.2 × turn energy)`, once a turn, not in a turn with a boost (and no boost
after guarding). Until the unit's next turn: damage ×0.6 (×0.5 on tank legs; a shield's `guard`
replaces it when lower), impact ×0.5.

### 1.4 Charge, melee, burst, salvo

- **Charge:** action `charge` pays the weapon's energy and marks the slot charged; the slot counts
  as fired that turn. Firing a charged weapon costs no energy; firing an uncharged charge weapon
  is refused (`notCharged`). A charge lasts until fired, until staggered, or (hand weapons) until
  the arms are destroyed. Heat and ammunition are paid on firing.
- **Melee:** refused (`needsBoost`) unless the unit boosted this turn. No ammunition.
- **Burst:** `traitPower` shots (twin stays as two).
- **Salvo:** one shot at every living enemy, each rolled; the aimed part on the chosen target,
  the core on the others. One ammunition, one energy cost, one heat.

### 1.5 Modules

Action `module` (painter and nothing else takes `target`). `uses` per battle, one activation a
turn, no energy cost.

| Effect | What it does |
|---|---|
| `coolant` | heat −power |
| `barrier` | absorbs the next `power` damage; gone at the start of the unit's next turn |
| `overclock` | energy +power now, heat +25 |
| `painter` | the target is painted for 3 of its own turns: +power to hit it |
| `nanites` | every working part +power% of its maximum |
| `shock` | every living enemy takes `power` impact and 15 damage to the core |
| `chaff` | −power to hit the unit until its next turn |
| `limiter` | damage ×(1 + power/100) for the rest of this turn, heat +30 |

### 1.6 Hit chance and damage, in full

```
hit    = accuracy + head aim + arms aim (hand weapons) + fcs.aim[type] + quad bonus (shoulder)
         + mods.aim + paint bonus (if target painted)
         - evasion(target) - chaff (if target has chaff)
         + PART_MODIFIER[part] × (1 - fcs.focus) - head loss penalty        clamp 10..95
evasion = 0 if legs gone or staggered, else mobility + mods.evasion + (boosted ? thrust : 0)
damage = weapon.damage × (1 - resist × (pierce ? .5 : 1)) × attacker.mods.damage × limiter
         × (target staggered ? 1.3 : 1) × (target guarding ? guard : 1)     min 1
         halved when the aimed part is already destroyed (passes to the core)
barrier absorbs before armour.
```

### 1.7 AI

Enemy, one action per call, first that applies: cool if the next shot would overheat; use a
module (barrier below 50% core, nanites below 40% average armour, others at once); boost when a
melee weapon is ready and energy covers boost + strike; fire a usable weapon (charged first);
charge an uncharged charge weapon; guard when energy is left, no boost was used and the bar is
half full or more; end turn. Skirmisher boost every third round stays.

Auto player (simulations, tests, the Auto Turn action): as today, plus the same handling of
charge, melee and guard, modules by simple thresholds, and high-impact weapons first.

### 1.8 Difficulty and Auto Turn

`settings.difficulty: 'story' | 'standard' | 'veteran'` multiplies enemy scale by 0.8, 1, 1.2.
Options screen. Default `standard`.

Battle action **Auto Turn**: the auto player plays the rest of this turn.

### 2.2 Paint

- `GameState.paint` (scheme id, `factory` by default).
- `src/ui/paint.ts`: twelve schemes, each a hue and saturation for the armour and a colour for
  lights and trim; ramps are generated from them. `recolour(pixels, scheme, info)` is pure:
  armour pixels are mapped by brightness onto the scheme's ramp, lights and trim onto the accent.
- What counts as armour and what as trim is decided per family when sprites are processed
  (`scripts/art/process.ts` writes `paint: { accent, lo, hi }` on every frame layer in
  `sprites.json`): hues that cover little of the family drawing are trim; `lo`/`hi` is the
  brightness range of the armour, so dark and light families take paint equally well.
- Weapons and shields keep their own colours.
- Painted sprites are made once per sprite and scheme, on first use, and cached.
- Garage: L1/R1 on the slot list steps through the schemes.
- Enemies can carry a scheme too (`EnemyDef.paint`): arena opponents, and later bosses and elites.

Loading needs every part still owned and free; otherwise it is refused and the screen says which
part is missing. The garage reaches them with LEFT/RIGHT from the slot list.

## Phase 3: Campaign 2.0

### 3.1 Story structure

Phases of a contract, in the order the story offers them: `intro` (one at a time), `faction` (a
client's second contract needs its first; all six before going on), `rival` (the first meeting
with Ashfall), `choice` (three offered, one taken), `path` (the chosen client's contracts, one at a
time), `finale` (the chosen client's; ends Act II), `audit` (Act III, one at a time), `verdict`
(two offered, one taken; ends the campaign). The patrol and open contracts are always there.
The campaign is complete when a `verdict` contract is done. A story without `verdict` contracts
(the small lists the tests use) is complete at its finale, as before.

Per playthrough: 2 + 6 + 1 + 1 + 3 + 1 + 3 + 1 = 18 story contracts; 30 are written.
Existing ids are kept (`intro-1`, `hadron-1`, `choice-lumen`, `finale-undertow`, ...).

Story: the three clients' war is Act I and II. Act III: every side insured its war with one
underwriter, Meridian Mutual, which now forecloses on all of Varn and sends its Collections
Division. The pilot's licence is among the collateral. Last choice: enforce the foreclosure, or
default on it with Ashfall. Six short epilogues (three clients by two verdicts).

### 3.2 Objectives

`Battle.objective` (absent: destroy everything):

| Type | Victory | Defeat (besides the mech being destroyed) |
|---|---|---|
| `destroy` | every enemy destroyed | round limit |
| `survive {rounds}` | the given number of rounds completed, or every enemy destroyed | |
| `protect {name, hp}` | every enemy destroyed | the asset reaches 0: it loses 1 for each living enemy at the start of every round after the first |
| `leader {target}` | that enemy destroyed | round limit |
| `disable {target}` | that enemy's legs and arms (those it has) destroyed while its core holds | its core destroyed |
| `driveOff {target, share}` | that enemy's core at or below the share of its armour | round limit |

### 3.3 Enemy abilities and bosses

`EnemyDef.abilities`: `aegis` (other enemies take a share of damage while it lives), `jam` (the
player aims worse while it lives), `mend` (repairs the most damaged ally instead of firing),
`bomb` (a fuse counts down on its turns; at zero it blows up on the player), `carrier` (launches
a unit every few rounds, up to three enemies in the battle).

`EnemyDef.phases`: when a boss's core falls to a share of its armour it changes behaviour: a new
AI profile, more damage, its balance restored, its module refilled, and a line of talk.

`EnemyDef.partNames` renames parts on screen (a machine has a hull and tracks, not a core and legs).

### 3.4 Ranks and the archive

A won contract gets a rank from a score: `100 - 5 per round over par - 70 x share of armour lost
at the end - 4 per field repair used`; S from 88, A from 72, B from 55, C from 35, else D. Par is
the sum over battles of `battle.par ?? 3 + 2 x enemies`. The best rank per contract is saved.
S pays 25% more, A 10% more. Completed story contracts can be played again from the archive for
30% pay; nothing in the story changes, the rank can improve.

### 3.5 Talk

`Battle.talk`: lines with a speaker and an optional portrait, shown in a box over the battle at
its start, at the start of a given round, at a boss's phase change, and after a win. Confirm goes
to the next line. Skipped entirely in fast (test) mode.

### 3.6 Scenes

Act openings and the endings are scenes: a title, a picture where there is one, and a few lines.
`GameState.scenes` lists the ones shown. The old `endingSeen` flag stays for the final ending.

### 3.7 Save version 7

Adds `ranks: {}` and `scenes: []`. A version 6 save that had finished the old campaign has not
finished the new one: `endingSeen` is reset so the real ending shows when it is earned.

### Stages

1. Objectives, abilities, phases in the rules, with the automatic player aware of objectives.
2. Story order, ranks, archive, scenes, save v7.
3. Screens: objective and asset in battle, talk box, phase flash, part names, rank on the
   settlement, archive list, scene screen.
4. Enemies, bosses and their art; portraits, backgrounds, stills.
5. The thirty contracts, messages, scenes, epilogues.
6. Balance by simulation for every route; open-contract costs for the new enemies.
7. Review, push, PR, APK.

### Review focus

1. An objective that can be neither won nor lost; a battle that ends twice; an objective checked
   on the wrong unit after a carrier adds one.
2. Old saves (v1 to v6) at every point of the old story continue sensibly in the new one.
3. Story order: a contract offered too early or never; a route that cannot be finished; the
   choice or the verdict offered twice.
4. Abilities: an aegis or jammer still counting after its unit is destroyed; a mender healing the
   dead; a bomb going off twice or after it was destroyed; a phase triggering more than once.
5. Ranks: a replay overwriting a better rank; the bonus paid on a lost contract.

- The field shakes on a heavy hit, a stagger, a blast, a destruction and a phase change.

- Weather per background: rain, dust, embers, drifting motes.
- The core bar of the player blinks when it is low.

### 4.4 Text that writes itself

`src/ui/typewriter.ts`. Talk lines and pages of story are typed out; the first press finishes the
text, the next one goes on. Briefings and messages type out too, without holding up their menus.

### 4.5 The title screen

A picture of Varn in the rain, the title with a glint passing over it, and the menu.

### 4.6 The settlement

The rank is stamped on, and the net amount counts up.

### 4.7 Sound and rumble

New sounds for a blast, a launch, a patch-up, a phase change, the off-field ally, a fuse, the
stamp and the typing. `Settings.rumble` (on by default, optional in old saves) vibrates the
handheld on the same moments the field shakes; an option turns it off.

### 4.8 The app icon

One generated picture, cut to the sizes Android wants.

## Phase 5: Meta

Branch `feat/meta`, on top of phase 4.

### 5.1 Save version 8, three games, a callsign

`GameState` gains `callsign` (1 to 8 of A-Z and 0-9; `PILOT` for older games), `stats` (counters,
see `src/core/stats.ts`), `medals` (ids earned), `met` (kinds of enemy met) and `drills` (training
passed). Three games are kept: the first under the old key, the others under `ironwage-save-2` and
`-3`; `ironwage-slot` remembers which was played last. The title offers Continue (the last one
played), New Game (pick a place; an occupied one asks first), Load Game (when more than one is
kept) and Options. A new game asks for the callsign on a grid of letters.

### 5.2 Record and medals

Each battle is tallied from its events (shots, hits, damage dealt and taken, kills, parts
destroyed, staggers, overheats, bombs defused, bosses beaten, rounds). About thirty medals, each
a condition on the game state, checked whenever the state is committed; a newly earned one is
announced by a toast. The pilot screen gets three pages: perks, medals, record.

### 5.3 Database

A screen with three pages: units (every kind of enemy met, with what it is weak to and what it
does), the world (entries that open as the story goes on) and the manual (how the game works).

### 5.4 Training

Ten drills, each one battle with a loaned mech that teaches one thing, with talk that explains
it. A drill costs nothing and pays once.

### 5.5 Arena

A mirror match against the pilot's own build, and five more opponents above the champion.

### 5.6 Credits

After an ending, once.

### Stages

1. State, storage, title, callsign. 2. Tally, medals, toasts, pilot pages. 3. Database.
4. Training. 5. Arena. 6. Credits. 7. Review, PR, APK.

## Changes made while building

Phase 1:

- The stock booster and fire control are real parts with a price of 100 (the validity check needs
  owned parts to have a vendor), weight 0 and drain 0.
- Part info shows a weapon's impact; weapons list their weight on the ammunition line.
- The weapon menu lists every fitted weapon and says why one cannot be used, instead of hiding it.
- Battle messages use the right verb for the player ("You boost", not "You boosts").
- End Turn sits above Auto Turn, so a turn with nothing left to do opens on End Turn.
- The salvo rack is `wpn-hornet-salvo` (Hornet Salvo), not `wpn-swarm-salvo`.
- The image ledger counts with the higher published token rates and counts failed requests too;
  its ceiling is $14, of which $2.50 is an estimate of everything made before it existed.
- The automatic player puts its hardest-pushing weapons first only against a target that will
  outlast the volley and that the volley can stagger; small targets are shot in slot order.
- With the new rules and no new parts on either side, simulated win rates of the story stayed in
  their earlier bands; the arena's top rank became easier and is retuned in phase 2.
- After the independent review: a unit that boosted to close in strikes with its blade before it
  fires anything else; a shock pulse goes through barrier, guard and stagger like any other hit;
  a build whose only usable weapon is a blade needs the energy for boost and strike together, and
  on legs that cannot boost a blade does not count as a weapon; losing the arms loses the charge
  of a hand weapon; the automatic player does not overclock into overheating; reverse-jointed legs
  have ordinary stability (the 0.9 was not in this plan); the weapon menu shows each weapon on two
  lines (name, then cost or the reason it cannot be used); the image ledger books a request before
  it is sent and refuses to run on a ledger it cannot read.

Phase 2:

- A blade strike spends the boost: the mech that closed in keeps none of the boost's evasion. Without
  this a light mech with a blade was almost never hit and hit hardest.
- Between the battles of a contract the crew refills half of every magazine (`FIELD_RESUPPLY`); the
  rounds are billed at the end. A build that lives on ammunition no longer runs dry in a third battle.
  The three finales carry a scale (1.2, 1.15, 1.3) to keep their difficulty where it was.
- The shared sprite palette has 64 colours (was 48), rebuilt with the Mantis drawing in it.
- Tracks are lower than legs: a legs sprite may carry `drop`, and everything above it is drawn that
  much lower. Only `legs-crawler` uses it (28 pixels).
- Paint info is also recorded on enemy sprites, each judged on its own.
- The paint ramp has seven stops and maps brightness through the family's low, median and high.
- In a duel the two mechs stand 12 pixels further apart.
- Arena: every opponent re-equipped and repainted; Brick gained a flare mortar; scales re-set by
  simulation (0.75 to 1.25).
- After the review: only a gun counts towards "never without a weapon" (a blade is no weapon on
  tracks, so the last gun cannot be sold and a pilot with nothing but blades is issued the rifle);
  the garage shows a saved build's own errors while it is highlighted; a saved build must be a
  whole build to be accepted from storage; free-standing legs take paint with the family their
  drawing was edited from; the shop compares damage and impact by the pull of the trigger (three
  shots of 20 beat one of 38); a row of comparison chips is at most 27 characters.

Phase 3:

- A battle can also have `support`: an ally who is not on the field and hits the strongest enemy
  once at the start of every round after the first. Ashfall fights this way in the third act.
- Phases can also `refit` a boss's weapons and module, and add to its evasion, aim and energy.
- Bombs and launches happen at the start of the unit's own turn, so a turn lost to overheating
  holds a fuse and a launch bay still.
- A volley stops as soon as the battle is decided: the shots left in a burst do not go on into the
  core of a machine that has just been disabled.
- The automatic player braces for a bomb about to go off and for a charged shot, shoots helpers
  (shield, repair, jammer, bomb) first, takes its stance first in a battle that only has to be
  outlasted, never uses a shock pulse near a machine wanted in one piece, and in such a battle
  clears the helpers before the limbs.
- Story order is by the furthest phase the pilot has done anything in, so a game older than part
  of the story is never sent back: one who had taken a side goes on with that client, one who had
  won the old war goes straight to the audit.
- Conditions for messages, stock and scenes also know `finale` and `verdict` (any of them); a
  message can be for one client's pilot only.
- The old endings became the close of the second act (`war-won-*` scenes). The real endings are
  six epilogues, one per verdict and client. A game that says the ending was seen is believed.
- The Overseer does not launch drones: a carrier that keeps launching shield drones made a fight
  nobody could finish. It keeps one shield drone close instead, and jams.
- Enemy mechs built from shop frames need a generator that feeds them: Ashfall, Sable and Krell
  were firing one weapon a turn until they got one.
- Part names on screen are at most five characters (the bar starts right after them).
- The seven Meridian parts (Auditor frame, Writ Rifle, Gavel, Decree Lance) are sold after any
  verdict: 112 parts on the shelf.
- Open contracts field the new units: support units never as the first pick, at most one a battle;
  named pilots, machines and Meridian's units belong to the story.
- Balance targets used (automatic player, 120 tries): second client contracts 55% and up with
  tier 1; the rival 75% with tier 2; war contracts 50 to 98% with tier 4; finales 35 to 95% with
  tier 3 and 60% up with tier 4; audit 45% up with tier 4; verdicts 25 to 90% with tier 4.
  Tier 3 has less firepower than tier 2 (two weapons a turn) and does badly where time matters.
- Par is set by hand for long fights (a named pilot 8 or 9 rounds, a machine 9 or 10, the last
  two 11 and 12).
- Images: 35 generated for this phase (one refused by the safety filter and reworded). Ledger
  total $3.74 of $14.
- After the review: Ashfall Unbound never fired a gun (a boost and the edge took all its energy),
  so it now fights with lance and rack and draws the edge only in its last phase, with 60 more
  energy to fire a gun in the turns it strikes; the contract's scale went from 1.25 to 1.06. A test
  now lets every enemy play two dozen turns and checks that it fires everything it carries. Tags in
  the battle panels are left out, least pressing first, when the row has no room (what a unit is
  goes before what is about to happen). The settlement's rows sit one line apart. A killing hit on
  a pilot being driven off leaves the core one point: they break away. A line of talk for a round
  the pilot sat out is said on their next turn. A game already in a later cycle is sold what the
  third act unlocks only once it has given a verdict (it holds a rank on one). Auto Turn keeps a
  salvo in its rack when the shots that go wide could finish a machine wanted in one piece. Backing
  out of a briefing leaves the cursor on that contract.
- Later cycles: enemies are 25% tougher per cycle (was 35%), and what has to be protected is
  sturdier by the same share. With 35% and no change to protected things the automatic player with
  the best build and six perk ranks won some second-cycle contracts 0 to 14 times in a hundred.
- Left as they are, on purpose: a light duellist build does badly in the two contracts that only
  have to be outlasted (saved builds are there for that); par does not bind in battles that are
  outlasted or protected.

Phase 4:

- The opening banner and the warning hold the battle still while they are up (about a second,

- Eleven new synthesised sounds. Images: one (the icon). Ledger total $3.75 of $14.
