# The Ledger Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** The pilots of an account get a face, an entrance, an announcement when their account opens, and a page in the database.

**Architecture:** The faces are sprites named by callsign (`GRUDGE_FACES`), carried by the words of an account and put on the lines its pilot speaks. The entrance is the one a boss has, opened by the app for the machine a notice is about, under the notice's own word. What is announced and what the page lists are pure functions of the saved game in `src/core/grudge.ts` (`accountBegun`, `ledger`); the app and the database screen only show them.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, Canvas 2D at 310x270, vitest, Playwright; the art scripts in `scripts/art/` for the portraits.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-23-ledger-design.md`

## Global Constraints

- No rule of battle and no figure of an account changes; the saved game keeps its shape (`grudge`, `accounts: string[]`).
- Pictures are made at the lowest quality, through the proxy named by `ART_PROXY_URL`; its address is never written into the repository. The round may spend half a dollar at the most; every picture is in `art/ledger.json`.
- No face is of a real person or of a character of another game.
- Every new text has its Dutch, within the room its kind has; interface strings in Dutch at most two letters longer than the English, with the same numbers in the same order.
- Nobody is listed before an account with them begins.

## Review Focus

1. A saved account whose callsign has no face (a name from another list): no picture is drawn where the face would be (in a fight the square has the mark every voice without a face has there; on the page there is nothing), and nothing breaks: the fight, the page and the announcement all go on.
2. A game that is loaded with an account already open: nothing is announced; and a meeting won, lost or walked away from announces nothing.
3. The page of an account with the longest machine name, the longest weapon name and every state (not met, met once, met twice; knows nothing, knows a weapon; due, one contract off, three off): every line of it is on the page, in English and Dutch.
4. A callsign that has come round again (closed before and open now, or closed twice): one row, and it says what is so now.
5. The entrance in a calm picture and in a hurried battle: as a boss's is there.

## The independent review

A reviewer who had seen none of the round's reasoning read the branch (`2533637..4dddb29`), looked at every picture, and drove the game itself through the states an account can be in (seventy contracts of one career, forty-two bands, every sentence of the page). Thirteen findings: one that had to be fixed and twelve small ones. Each was checked against the code before anything was changed; all thirteen were so.

1. **The page said "You have not met by name yet" after a meeting lost or walked away from**, and "met once, and won" after a second one lost. An account counts the meetings won, and a meeting lost leaves it as it was; the lines spoke of meetings held. They count what the account counts now: `Meetings won: none | one | two of the three that close the account.` The guide and the README say won as well. Tests lose a first, a second and a third meeting and walk away from one, and read the page before and after: word for word the same. (The design asked for "how many meetings there have been", which the saved game does not know; it asks for the meetings won now.)
2. **"Their notice is out: it is among the open contracts" while it was not on offer**: a pilot whose machine may not deploy is offered the patrol alone. The line no longer says where the notice is: `Their notice is out, and stays out until you win it.`
3. **A callsign come round again.** The page of the open one is covered by 1. The closed line counted three meetings of a callsign that may have been closed twice: it counts nothing now. The count at the foot (`ACCOUNTS n`) is left as the number of rows on the page, as `KNOWN` beside it is, where the pilot's record counts the accounts closed: the two differ only once a seventeenth account is closed.
4. **Wording.** How an account began read as what is so now, and the sentence after it then said otherwise: each of those lines says `once`. The Dutch that was English word for word ("bij naam ontmoet", "jij won beide") went with the lines of 1, and the stressed "jij" is "je".
5. **The list did not say which rows are accounts, or which is open.** A row has `OPEN` or `CLOSED` (Dutch `DICHT`) behind its callsign, and the guide says so.
6. **A callsign with no face has the terminal's mark in a fight**, where this plan's Review Focus said nothing is drawn. The mark is what every voice without a face has there, and is kept; the line of the focus is corrected, and a test holds both: the mark and no picture in the fight, nothing at all on the page.
7. **The guide and the README said "databank"** where the screen says Database in both languages. Both say Database now, and the guide's "over de afrekening" is "bij de afrekening".
8. **No test tied the sixteen faces to files.** One does: each a PNG of 56 by 56 in `public/sprites`, no two the same picture, each kept as it was made, at the lowest quality, from a description of "an original character".
9. **No test saw a face being drawn.** With a store whose pictures say which they are, tests see theirs in the box at its own size once the band has gone (and none while it is up), beside their last word as their machine falls, nobody's but theirs and Dispatch's through a whole meeting, and on the page under the callsign.
10. **Titles that claimed more than they held.** "Under every callsign" flies all sixteen. The "hurried" game was the one a test runs, which no player has: it is called that, and the player's option for faster battles has a test of its own (the band stays up exactly as long). A meeting lost and one walked away from are flown through the app. The browser test asks who comes in and under what word, not only that a banner is up.
11. **A callsign as long as a saved game may hold overran its row.** Only a file made elsewhere can have one. Its row is cut to ten letters, the last of them a stop; its page has it whole. (The announcement cannot have one: it is only made for a callsign the game has just given.)
12. **Names and duplication.** The option that names who comes in on the band says it may be the pilot of an account. What a machine and a weapon are called is asked of the app in one place. `ledger` keeps its name, with a line that says it is not the ledger of what the pictures cost.
13. **Two faces nearest a familiar type**: Stub's flat black fringe and Mercer's coat. Stub was made again from another description (a knitted cap and a week of stubble), for 0.84 cent. Mercer is kept: a bald head, a goatee and a coat with piping are nobody in particular.

The endurance test found one thing of its own, in itself: with the band and the face added to what its two players with an account look for, both failed. It asked for the band only after asking whether a press would be passed over, and while the band is up one always is (the line under it holds presses off). It looks before anything else now.
