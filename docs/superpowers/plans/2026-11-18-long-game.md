# The Long Game Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Make the cycles after the first worth playing and fair: a bench that turns credits into better parts, strength that stops growing where the bench does, three contracts that can be flown for the other side, and a picture for every find.

**Architecture:** Bench work is a record in the saved game (`tuning`: part id to step) that the battle reads through the player's modifiers; no part gets a second id. A later cycle's strength is one function of the cycle. The other offer is a decision inside a contract, kept with the story's other decisions. Pictures are sprites named after the find.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, Canvas 2D at 310x270, vitest, Playwright, the art pipeline in `scripts/art`.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-18-long-game-design.md`

## Global Constraints

- A saved game from before this round loads and stays valid.
- A first cycle flown without the bench plays exactly as before: no balance test of the story changes.
- Every new English text has its Dutch inside the room its kind has (`tests/content/dutch.test.ts`).
- Image spend this round: at most 1 dollar, low quality only; the proxy's address and key are never committed.
- Nothing is merged without the user's word. Each phase: its own branch on the one before, a draft PR, an APK.

## Review Focus

- A pilot who sells a tuned part and buys it back: the work is still there, and nothing was paid twice.
- A lent or stock frame that shares a part with the pilot's own: it is not tuned.
- The bench's limit when the story starts over: one more step is on offer, and no step is lost.
- An other offer that is accepted and then lost or left: nothing is remembered, and the first offer is still there.
- A saved game whose `tuning` names a part that does not exist, a step past the limit, or a number that is no number.

## A1: The bench

**Files:** `src/core/tuning.ts` (new), `src/core/game.ts` (the field and its validation), `src/core/battle.ts` (the three places a step counts), `src/ui/garageFlow.ts`, `src/ui/garageScreen.ts`, `src/ui/partInfo.ts`, `src/ui/app.ts` (the player's modifiers; the mirror match), `src/content/strings.ts`, `src/content/nl/strings.ts`, `src/content/codex.ts` (a manual topic), `docs/handleiding.md`; tests in `tests/core/tuning.test.ts` (new), `tests/core/battle*.test.ts`, `tests/ui/garage*.test.ts`, `tests/ui/app*.test.ts`.

**Interfaces:**
- `GameState.tuning?: Record<string, number>` (part id to step, 1 to 5; absent or empty for none).
- `TUNE_STEP = 0.05`, `TUNE_MAX = 5`, `tuneCap(cycle) = min(TUNE_MAX, 2 + cycle)`.
- `tunable(part): boolean` (frame parts, weapons, generators). `stepOf(tuning, id): number`. `tuneCost(part, step) = part.price * step`.
- `checkTune(state, id, catalog): 'unknown' | 'notOwned' | 'notTunable' | 'limit' | 'credits' | null`; `tune(state, id, catalog): GameState` (pays, one step up; throws when refused).
- `tunedPart(part, step)`: the part with its armour, damage or output as tuned (rounded), for the garage's numbers. `tunedCatalog(catalog, tuning)`: the same ids, the pilot's parts as tuned.
- `UnitMods.tune?: Record<string, number>`: read where a unit's armour is set, where a shot's damage is worked out, and where a turn's energy is.

- [x] Tests first (`tuning.test.ts`): the cost of each step; the cap by cycle; every refusal; a step paid for and kept through a sale and a new cycle; `tunedPart` for each kind and for a kind that is not tuned.
- [x] Tests first (battle): with `mods.tune` a frame part has a twentieth more armour a step, a weapon's hit a twentieth more damage a step, a turn a twentieth more of the generator's output a step; an enemy with the same part ids is as before.
- [x] `tuning.ts`, the field, its validation (a record of at most 256 known-shaped ids to whole steps 1 to 5); `battle.ts`.
- [x] The app: the pilot's own build flies tuned; a loaner and the Gauntlet's frame do not; the mirror match's copy does.
- [x] The garage: R1 on an owned part opens the bench (what a step adds, what it costs, why not); the list and the panel show the step and the tuned numbers; Dutch.
- [x] A manual topic, the guide, all tests, the browser tests that touch the garage, screenshots, an APK.

Notes on what A1 became:

- A step is read in three places of a battle and nowhere else: where a unit's armour is set, where a shot's damage is worked out (so the preview of a shot promises what the shot does), and where a turn's energy is. No part has a second id, so nothing that goes by a part's id (its picture, its name, its Dutch) had to learn about the bench.
- The garage reads the catalogue through `tunedCatalog`: the same ids, the pilot's parts as tuned. Whether a build may deploy is judged by the same view, so a generator the bench has worked on counts for what it now gives.
- The shop shows parts as the factory makes them, and the battle's own lists name weapons without their step: both are short of room, and the garage is where a part is judged.
- Two medals (the first step; a part at the last step) and a topic of the manual. The first step earning a medal that pays is why the tests read the credits off the game rather than off the price.
- A browser test (`e2e/long.spec.ts`) puts a part on the bench, pays for two steps, and flies the result.
