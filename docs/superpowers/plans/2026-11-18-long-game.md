# The Long Game Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Make the cycles after the first worth playing and fair: a bench that turns credits into better parts, strength that stops growing where the bench does, three contracts that can be flown for the other side, and a picture for every find.

**Architecture:** Bench work is a record in the saved game (`tuning`: part id to step) that the battle reads through the player's modifiers; no part gets a second id. A later cycle's strength is one function of the cycle. The other offer is a decision inside a contract, kept with the story's other decisions. Pictures are sprites named after the find.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, Canvas 2D at 310x270, vitest, Playwright, the art pipeline in `scripts/art`.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-18-long-game-design.md`

## Global Constraints

- A saved game from before this round loads and stays valid.
- A first cycle flown without the bench plays exactly as before: no balance test of the story changes.
- Every new English text has its Dutch inside the room its kind has (`tests/content/dutch.test.ts`).
- Image spend this round: at most 1 dollar, low quality only; the proxy's address and key are never committed.
- Nothing is merged without the user's word. Each phase: its own branch on the one before, a draft PR, an APK.

## Review Focus

- A pilot who sells a tuned part and buys it back: the work is still there, and nothing was paid twice.
- A lent or stock frame that shares a part with the pilot's own: it is not tuned.
- The bench's limit when the story starts over: one more step is on offer, and no step is lost.
- An other offer that is accepted and then lost or left: nothing is remembered, and the first offer is still there.
- A saved game whose `tuning` names a part that does not exist, a step past the limit, or a number that is no number.

## A1: The bench

**Files:** `src/core/tuning.ts` (new), `src/core/game.ts` (the field and its validation), `src/core/battle.ts` (the three places a step counts), `src/ui/garageFlow.ts`, `src/ui/garageScreen.ts`, `src/ui/partInfo.ts`, `src/ui/app.ts` (the player's modifiers; the mirror match), `src/content/strings.ts`, `src/content/nl/strings.ts`, `src/content/codex.ts` (a manual topic), `docs/handleiding.md`; tests in `tests/core/tuning.test.ts` (new), `tests/core/battle*.test.ts`, `tests/ui/garage*.test.ts`, `tests/ui/app*.test.ts`.

**Interfaces:**
- `GameState.tuning?: Record<string, number>` (part id to step, 1 to 5; absent or empty for none).
- `TUNE_STEP = 0.05`, `TUNE_MAX = 5`, `tuneCap(cycle) = min(TUNE_MAX, 2 + cycle)`.
- `tunable(part): boolean` (frame parts, weapons, generators). `stepOf(tuning, id): number`. `tuneCost(part, step) = part.price * step`.
- `checkTune(state, id, catalog): 'unknown' | 'notOwned' | 'notTunable' | 'limit' | 'credits' | null`; `tune(state, id, catalog): GameState` (pays, one step up; throws when refused).
- `tunedPart(part, step)`: the part with its armour, damage or output as tuned (rounded), for the garage's numbers. `tunedCatalog(catalog, tuning)`: the same ids, the pilot's parts as tuned.
- `UnitMods.tune?: Record<string, number>`: read where a unit's armour is set, where a shot's damage is worked out, and where a turn's energy is.

- [x] Tests first (`tuning.test.ts`): the cost of each step; the cap by cycle; every refusal; a step paid for and kept through a sale and a new cycle; `tunedPart` for each kind and for a kind that is not tuned.
- [x] Tests first (battle): with `mods.tune` a frame part has a twentieth more armour a step, a weapon's hit a twentieth more damage a step, a turn a twentieth more of the generator's output a step; an enemy with the same part ids is as before.
- [x] `tuning.ts`, the field, its validation (a record of at most 256 known-shaped ids to whole steps 1 to 5); `battle.ts`.
- [x] The app: the pilot's own build flies tuned; a loaner and the Gauntlet's frame do not; the mirror match's copy does.
- [x] The garage: R1 on an owned part opens the bench (what a step adds, what it costs, why not); the list and the panel show the step and the tuned numbers; Dutch.
- [x] A manual topic, the guide, all tests, the browser tests that touch the garage, screenshots, an APK.

Notes on what A1 became:

- A step is read in three places of a battle and nowhere else: where a unit's armour is set, where a shot's damage is worked out (so the preview of a shot promises what the shot does), and where a turn's energy is. No part has a second id, so nothing that goes by a part's id (its picture, its name, its Dutch) had to learn about the bench.
- The garage reads the catalogue through `tunedCatalog`: the same ids, the pilot's parts as tuned. Whether a build may deploy is judged by the same view, so a generator the bench has worked on counts for what it now gives.
- The battle's own lists name weapons without their step: they are short of room, and the garage is where a part is judged. The shop showed parts as the factory makes them until the review: a part worked on is now shown there as it would come off the shelf, and held against what is worn as the bench left that (see A5).
- Two medals (the first step; a part at the last step) and a topic of the manual. The first step earning a medal that pays is why the tests read the credits off the game rather than off the price.
- A browser test (`e2e/long.spec.ts`) puts a part on the bench, pays for two steps, and flies the result.

The first two rows were wrong as this table was first written, and were found so in the review: the harness scaled the enemies and left what has to be protected as the first telling has it, where the game grows it by as much (`forCycle`, before this round and now). Whole careers, with everything the game offers: eight cycles on Standard finish with no contract lost twice running and the bench at its limit on all seven parts it takes; five cycles on Veteran and on Story finish; six cycles on Standard finish without the bench.

## A3: The other offer

**Files:** `src/core/campaign.ts` (`Mission.other`, `otherOffer`, what winning records), `src/ui/briefingScreen.ts`, `src/ui/app.ts`, `src/content/missions.ts` (three offers, three letters), `src/content/sidework.ts` (a favour's condition), `src/content/talks.ts` (what is remembered), `src/content/finds.ts` (three conditions, two clues), `src/content/nl/*`, `scripts/careerBot.ts`, `scripts/career.ts`, `docs/handleiding.md`; tests in `tests/content/offers.test.ts` (new), `tests/core/balance4.test.ts` (new), `tests/scripts/career.test.ts`, `tests/helpers/conditions.ts`, `e2e/long.spec.ts`.

**Interfaces:**
- `Mission.other?: { when: Condition; question: string; answer: string; client: string; portrait?: string; reward: number; briefing: string[]; debrief: string; battles: Battle[]; scale?: number }`, and `Mission.turned?: { question: string; answer: string }` on the contract as the other side offers it.
- `otherOffer(state, mission, missions): Mission | null`: the contract as the other side offers it (same id), when its time has come and the contract is not done. Not made for a contract played again from the archive.
- `forCycle` grows the other offer as it grows the contract: pay, strength, and what has to be kept alive.
- `App.startMission(missionId, replay = false, wingmanId = null, other = false)`; the settlement records `decide(state, question, answer)` before the contract is completed; losing or leaving records nothing.
- `CareerOptions.offers?: boolean`, `CareerReport.offers: string[]`; `npm run career -- --offers`.

- [x] Tests first: not offered in a first cycle; offered in a later one until the contract is done; winning records the answer and completes the contract; the story's next phase opens; the archive replays the first offer.
- [x] The three offers of the spec's table, their letters and what is remembered; the two papers' conditions; the third verdict waits for `!chose:lights=dark`.
- [x] The briefing: "Other offer" and back; the pay and the client shown are the offer's.
- [x] The career bot can be told to take them; a career that takes all three finishes.
- [x] Dutch, all tests, a browser test, screenshots, an APK.

Notes on what A3 became:

- The other offer is the same contract with other terms: the same id, title and place in the story, so everything that goes by a contract's id (what comes after it, its rank, the archive) needed nothing new. What winning it means is an answer kept with the story's other answers (`seam=stand`, `train=weigh`, `lights=dark`), which is why letters, finds, a favour, the third verdict and the page at the end could all be told about it with the conditions they already had. The answers are a telling's: the next one starts clean.
- The briefing opens on the first offer with the cursor on Accept, as every briefing does, and has one more row. The header says there are two; on the other one it says that taking it is remembered. Whoever was hired for the first offer is not carried to the other, where the fight may have no room for them (the tank of Seam Nine fires for the pilot in both battles of the first one).
- The weighbridge was first something to protect. Measured with the automatic player in four frames (wins in a hundred, second telling then the peak): a tank and a turret against a scale of 9 gave 31 55 100 54 and 30 20 99 32; a tank, a turret and a drone against 14 gave 62 73 99 53 and 64 73 99 20; two turrets and a drone against 10 gave 16 7 95 8 and 0 0 85 0. What decided those was whether the automatic player happened to shoot the right machine first, not the frame. It became a hold-out: five rounds against a guard at twice its strength (Bulwark, tank, turret), 90 98 92 100 and 60 87 65 100 bare, where the first offer gives 78 100 100 95 and 73 100 100 90. A pilot who only fires does not last it; one who guards does.
- With a grown pilot's perks all three are won 85 times in a hundred or more on Standard and 68 or more on Veteran. Lumen's is the easiest fight in the game for its pay, and is meant to be: what it costs is Tamsin's thanks and favour, and the third verdict of that telling. (The rhyme was meant to be part of the cost and cannot be: nobody is made the offer who does not hold it already. The review found that; the condition is gone.)
- Two holes in tests were found by looking at the screen. The offers' briefings were held to a count of lines and not to the dots the screen has (a gap between paragraphs is four of them): the weighbridge's last line fell off the panel in both languages. And a test "in Dutch" that makes a game is English unless the saved game says Dutch, because a game sets the language when it loads; the test now asks for the Dutch words by name.
- Forty-three small faults were put into the new code one at a time (the rule, the app, the briefing, the conditions of letters, finds, the favour and the third verdict, the translation, the bot). Forty-two were caught as the tests stood; the one that was not moved four rows of the briefing's menu half a line, and the test now says where the rows sit.
- The bot's careers: a second cycle that takes all three finishes on Standard; on Veteran they are taken in the second and the third cycle and it finishes; the career that reaches the third verdict stops short of it once Lumen's money is taken, with the story's other contracts all flown.

## A4: A picture for every find

**Files:** `scripts/art/jobs.ts`, `art/raw/find-*`, `public/sprites/find-*`, `src/content/sprites.json`, `art/ledger.json`, `src/core/codex.ts` (`Find.picture`), `src/content/finds.ts`, `src/ui/databaseScreen.ts`, `docs/handleiding.md`; tests in `tests/ui/finds.test.ts`, `e2e/long.spec.ts`.

**Interfaces:**
- `Find.picture: string`: a paper's is `find-<its id>`; a check's is one of three bare brasses, `find-check-0` (kept dry), `find-check-1` (green from the water), `find-check-2` (black from a fire).
- `FIND_PICTURE = 56`, `FIND_SOURCE_CHARS` (the characters to a line beside the picture). The page's detail gains `picture` and, on a check, `stamp` (its number); one not found has neither.

- [x] A trial of three pictures at the page's size before the rest is made: a paper, a check, an object.
- [x] The pictures (twenty-four papers and three bare brasses; the game stamps a check's number), the ledger, the page: the picture above the text, none for a find not found, every text still on the page.
- [x] All tests, screenshots, an APK.

Notes on what A4 became:

- The trial (the rule book, the brass plate, a bare check) showed that a thing lying there reads well at fifty-six dots and that writing does not: so every paper's picture is the paper as a thing (wet, folded, stamped, cut with a knife), with rows of marks and no letters. What it says stays in words, on the page.
- A check is one of three pictures of bare brass, chosen by where the check has been, and the page stamps the number on it: dark, with a light edge one dot down and to the right. A number in a generated picture cannot be trusted; this one is the find's own.
- The page: the picture under the name, the size it was made, as a face is; where the find comes from beside it, in short lines; the words below. Twelve lines of words still fit, in both languages, and a test walks all forty pages in each. One not found is as it was: three marks, that it is not found, and the clue.
- Two pictures were made again after looking at them. The rhyme's wall had ten chalk circles where the rhyme says nine: it is now three rows of three. The old licence had a clear face in its photo, which would have given a face to somebody the story does not show: the photo is now faded to nothing.
- Twenty-nine requests at low quality: $0.2443 of the dollar this round may spend. The ledger stands at $5.4680 of $14.
- Fifteen small faults put into the page and into the finds one at a time (no picture, a picture for one not found, a stamp on a paper, the stamp over the hole or off the middle, words over the picture, the brasses swapped) are all caught by the tests.
