# The Works Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Two new frames for the end of the game (the Sirocco, which runs hot, and the Solstice, which keeps the energy it does not spend), each met in a duel with one of the yard's works pilots before it can be bought.

**Architecture:** What each frame does is a figure on its core, as a field is on a Sterling's: `heatLimit` and `buffer`. A unit is given both when it is made (`UnitState.heatLimit`, `UnitState.buffer`), and everything that used the constant `HEAT_LIMIT` for a unit asks the unit. The buffer is settled where a turn ends and where the next begins. The duels are two more contracts of the series `duel`; a works pilot is an `EnemyDef` like the two swords, and the one that holds its fire has an ability for it that the reader of intents knows.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, Canvas 2D at 310 x 270, vitest, Playwright.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-20-works-design.md`

## Global Constraints

- Sirocco: `core-sirocco` has `heatLimit: 130`. Every other machine's limit is `HEAT_LIMIT = 100`, which stays the name of the usual one.
- Solstice: `core-solstice` has `buffer: 50`: energy left at the end of the unit's turn is kept, up to `floor(turnEnergyOf x 50 / 100)`, and added to the next turn's energy. A lost turn keeps what was kept. A drain and a stagger's half come off the turn's own share.
- An overheat is unchanged: past the limit, the next turn is lost and the heat comes down to `HEAT_AFTER_OVERHEAT`.
- A redline's heat counts up to the firing machine's own limit.
- The duels: `duel-3` (Kiln, Sirocco) and `duel-4` (Vesper, Solstice), series `duel`, `when: 'verdict'`, after `duel-2` in the list. The frames' parts open with `won:duel-3` and `won:duel-4`. Neither frame is bespoke.
- Vesper's machine fires only on rounds that divide by two (`abilities.hold: 2`); on the others it guards if it can, and its intent reads as holding.
- No frame, weapon or fight that exists changes; no balance test of what exists changes.
- A part's line is at most `PART_LINE_CHARS` letters; every new text has its Dutch; an interface string in Dutch is at most two letters longer than the English and carries the same numbers in the same order.
- Pictures at quality `low` only, through the two image functions and nothing else; the proxy's address is never written into the repository.
- Names and looks are this game's own.

## Review Focus

- Every reader of the heat limit: the overheat itself, a redline's cap, the game's pilot (what fits, when it vents, the coolant), the weapon row's warning, the bar's length and colour, the glow of a hot machine on the field, an enemy's limit in a spar or the mirror. One that still reads the constant is a Sirocco that overheats at 100 on one screen and not on another.
- The buffer across a lost turn, a stagger, a drain, Second Wind, a volley whose kill gives energy back, a battle's end and the next battle of the same contract (nothing is carried between battles): never more kept than the cap, never energy from nowhere.
- A Sirocco's or a Solstice's core on another frame, and another core on theirs: what the core does goes with the core.
- The machine that holds: what it is about to do is what it does; a stagger or an overheat in a holding round; the mirror of a pilot who flies a Solstice does not hold (the habit is the works pilot's, not the frame's).
- The new lines in Dutch in the shop's panel with the comparison under them; the heat figure with a limit beside it in the battle's panel.

### What the review found

A reader who had not written any of it read the branch as it stood after the final check and came back with
eight findings. Each was checked against the game before anything was changed; all eight held.

1. The game's own pilot braced for any machine that had kept energy. Against the mirror of a Solstice with two
   blades, which never fires what it kept as one shot, it guarded every turn and never struck: no win in thirty.
   It braces now only for kept energy that is about to be fired as a surge (`looming` in `src/core/ai.ts`). The
   fourth duel is flown as it was (64, 77 and 74 in a hundred for the three reference builds), and against four
   Solstice builds in the mirror the pilot does what a pilot blind to kept energy does.
2. to 4. A machine's next move is written beside its name once it is aimed at, and nowhere else; the guide said
   HOLDS stood over the machine, and what did stand over Vesper when it was about to fire was GUARD. What a
   machine has kept is now written over it for as long as it is about to be fired as a surge (`+180 EN`,
   blinking, in place of the guard), and is a tag in the row beside its name. The briefing, the dispatcher's line
   and the guide speak of what stands over the machine.
5. The manual and the README said the frames are won. They are bought, once their duel is won: both say so now.
6. to 8. Eight Dutch sentences that read as translated were written again.

Twenty-seven small faults went into the lines the review changed (what makes kept energy loom, the round of a
unit's next turn, the mark over a machine and the tag beside its name). Two were not caught: the colour of the tag,
and that it is the first to make way in a row with no room for everything. Each got a test; all twenty-seven are
caught now.

One thing about how the round was run. "No balance test of what exists changes" is one of the constraints above,
and the Gauntlet's pinned sample did change: of its forty runs, between 17 and 25 reach the tenth floor where it
said between 20 and 28. The sample changed, not the balance: the eight new parts are offered in a run like any
other part, so the same forty seeds are other runs. Over four hundred runs a run gets 8.9 floors deep with the
eight parts on offer and 8.8 without (180 reaching the tenth for 171), which is less than four hundred runs differ
by. The same was done, and written beside the test, when the ten weapons of the round before went on offer.
