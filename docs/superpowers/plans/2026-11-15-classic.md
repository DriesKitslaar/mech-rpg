# IRONWAGE: from a complete game to one worth remembering

The owner asked for everything that still separates the game from a classic, except playing it
themselves: motion, music, depth in battle, story and characters, size, and finish. They decide
nothing further; decisions are made here and written down. Game text stays English unless the
player picks Dutch (phase F). Total image spend stays under EUR 20 (ledger ceiling $14; $3.76
spent; low quality only, under a cent an image).

## Phase A: Motion

Mechs are paper dolls of five layers, so they can move without one new picture.

1. A pose for a mech: an offset and a small turn per layer, blended over time (`src/ui/pose.ts`,
   pure). Poses: idle (breathing, weight shifting), fire (the firing arm kicks back, the torso
   rocks), strike (a lunge across the field and back), boost (lean and dash, afterimages), guard
   (crouch behind the shield arm), hit (flinch away from the shot), stagger (sag and sway),
   overheat (slump, steam), part lost (the layer drops away and is gone), destroyed (the layers
   fly apart).
2. Enemies that are one picture move too: drones bob and bank, turrets kick, tracks rumble, bosses
   shudder when a phase changes; a destroyed one breaks into falling pieces cut from its picture.
3. Particles (`src/ui/particles.ts`, pure, seeded): muzzle flashes by damage type, tracers and
   beams that travel, shell casings, sparks where a shot lands, smoke from damaged parts, heat
   shimmer, explosions with debris, dust under a dash.
4. The field: parallax in the background while a mech dashes, a flash and a held frame on a
   killing blow, the camera easing rather than jumping.
5. The hub, garage and arena use the idle pose; the title's mech turns its head.
6. Fast Battles keep the motion and shorten it; the test mode (`fast=1`) has none.

## Phase B: Music and sound

1. A theme for each client and one for the rival, played in their contracts; a leitmotif shared by
   the title, the hub and the ending so the game has a tune of its own.
2. Songs get sections (intro, verse, lift, break) instead of one loop, and the battle song steps up
   when the pilot's core is failing or a boss changes phase.
3. Effects in layers: a shot is a click, a body and a tail; weapons sound like their kind and size.
4. Every song and effect stays measured (`e2e/audio.spec.ts`), and a mix test keeps a battle's
   loudest second under full scale.

## Phase C: Depth in battle

1. Range: every battle is fought at close, mid or far; weapons have a range they like, a dash
   closes or opens it, and enemies want their own. Blades need close; rails want far.
2. A wingman: from the second act a second pilot can be hired for a contract (a share of the fee),
   with three orders (focus my target, cover me, hold fire). They can be lost.
3. Terrain: each background has one rule (rain cools, sand jams, the foundry heats, the sluice
   slows legs) said in the briefing and on the field.
4. Enemy intent: what each enemy means to do next turn is shown over it, so a turn is a decision.

## Phase D: Story and characters

1. Conversations: scenes of two or three people talking, portrait beside each line, between
   contracts and inside them; each client, the rival and Dispatch get an arc with a turn in it.
2. Choices that are remembered: six smaller decisions across the acts (spare or finish, report or
   keep, whom to warn) that change later lines, a contract each, and the epilogues.
3. Bonds: the three named pilots can be made an ally or an enemy; an ally can be the wingman.
4. More pictures for the turns of the story.

## Phase E: Size

1. Twelve more story contracts (two side stories per client) and four kinds not yet seen: a chase,
   an ambush in waves, a duel under terms, a raid with a countdown.
2. Eight more kinds of enemy and two more bosses, six more backgrounds.
3. A second ladder: tournaments with rules (no repairs, one weapon, heat doubled).
4. Bounties: named targets that turn up in open contracts.

## Phase F: Finish

1. Dutch as a language for all game text, chosen in the options.
2. First-time prompts in the first three contracts, pointing at the button to press.
3. Key pictures redone where low quality shows.
4. A last independent review of the whole, and the summary.

## Changes made while building

Phase A:

- Motion is all arithmetic on the layers that were already there: no picture was added.
- Not done: parallax behind a dash and a head that turns on the title (the title has no mech).

Phase B:

- The songs already had sections and a shared motif, so that part of the plan was done before it
  was written. What was missing was a sound of its own for each side.
- The tense layer is a tick on every off step from the playing song's own drum kit, added by the
  player, so it needs nothing written into each song.
- Not done: effects in layers by weapon size. Nobody here can hear them, and the measured levels
  leave little room to stack more on a shot.

Phase C:

- Not done: range bands and terrain rules. Both change every number the balance was tuned on (the
  career tests hold three roads on three difficulties within measured limits), and nobody can play
  the result here to judge it. What was built adds decisions without moving those numbers: a
  wingman is a choice the player makes and pays for, and intent only shows what was already true.
- The wingman has no orders and cannot be lost: they stay off the field, as the allies of the
  story already did.

Phase D:

- Conversations are scenes with `talk` and `choice`; they come up on the way into the hub like the
  pages of the acts. A pilot loaded from an older save meets the ones whose moment has not passed.
- Six decisions, as planned. They change who flies the pilot's wing and at what share, pay on the
  spot in two cases, and are written up on two pages before the epilogue. Not done: a contract for
  each decision (new contracts are phase E) and conversations inside a battle beyond the lines
  enemies already speak.
- `choices` is an optional field of the save, so the save version did not change.
