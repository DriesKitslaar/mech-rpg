# Own Colours

## Why

The pilot's machine is what the game is about, and paint is how it becomes theirs. There are twelve
paints that come ready, seven that are taken from the arena's masters and one from the keeper of
the eighth seat. A pilot who wants a machine of their own colours (a white frame with blue lights,
a black one with red) cannot have it: the nearest ready paint is somebody else's idea.

The owner asked for mechs that look the part, named a white machine as the one they like best, and
left it to me to decide what is fun. In games of this kind a great deal of a player's time goes
into the paint shop, and none of it costs the game its balance. This round gives the pilot one.

## Goals

1. **A paint of the pilot's own mixing.** Five things are theirs to set: the colour of the armour,
   how strong that colour is, how light or dark it is, the colour of the lights and trim, and how
   hard those burn.
2. **Three mixes are kept**, each as it was left, and each is worn like any other paint: in the
   garage, in the hangar, in a battle from behind and in the launch.
3. **Mixed where the machine stands.** A page of the garage: the machine beside it changes with
   every press.
4. An APK after every step.

## Not goals

- No painting of one part differently from another, no patterns, no emblems. (Emblems may be a
  round of their own.)
- Weapons keep their own colours, as they do under every paint now.
- No colour typed in as numbers, and no more than three kept mixes.
- It costs nothing and is there from the first visit to the garage.
- No new room in the hangar and no new button.

## What would make it a failure

- A machine that cannot be read in a fight: so dark that it is lost on a dark field, so pale that
  a hit's flash does not show. The lightest and darkest a mix can be are no lighter and no darker
  than the ready paints already go.
- A handheld that fills up while its pilot tries colours: every press makes new pictures, and they
  must not all be kept.
- A kept game that will not open because of a mix in it that is not right.
- A page whose rows cannot be read, or a strip of colours in which the chosen one cannot be found.
- The masters' paints made worthless. They stay what they are by name: a mix is called MIX, and
  only the holder of a crown has that master's paint under its own name.

## How it works

### What a mix is

Five steps, each a whole number:

| | Steps | What it sets |
|---|---|---|
| Armour | 24 | The armour's colour: round the wheel in steps of fifteen degrees. |
| Finish | 4 | How strong the colour is: Steel (hardly any), Matte, Rich, Vivid. |
| Shade | 5 | How light or dark: Pale, Light, Mid, Dark, Deep. |
| Trim | 24 | The colour of lights and trim. |
| Glow | 3 | How hard they burn: Soft, Clear, Bright. |

Steel and Pale is a white machine; Steel and Deep a black one. That is 34,560 paints.

A mix is a paint like any other to everything that draws a machine: its name as a paint says what
it is (`mix1-14-3-2-13-2`: which of the three, then the five steps), so whatever is handed a paint
can colour a machine with it without being told anything else.

### The three that are kept

MIX 1, MIX 2 and MIX 3 are always there, after every other paint the pilot may wear. Until they
are touched they are three that were mixed for the pilot: a white machine with cyan lights, a
black one with red, and navy with gold. Changing a step changes that mix and is kept at once, as
every change in the garage is.

### The page

The garage's panel has had two pages: the parts, and the saved builds. It gets a third, and the
three stand in a ring: PAINT, PARTS, BUILDS. Left and right go round it. (Left on the parts used
to open the saved builds, as right did: it opens the paint now, and the foot of the screen says
which way each is.)

The page has six rows:

- **PAINT**: the paint the machine wears. L1 and R1 go through them all, as they do on the parts
  page.
- **ARMOUR**, **FINISH**, **SHADE**, **TRIM**, **GLOW**: each a strip of its steps in the colours
  they would give, with the one chosen marked, and for the three that have names the name. L1 and
  R1 change the row under the cursor, round and round. They are dim, and do nothing, while the
  machine wears a paint that is not a mix: a line under them says to choose MIX 1, 2 or 3.

### Against a copy of itself

The arena's mirror and a spar with a saved build put the pilot's own machine on the other side, in
obsidian so that the two are told apart (in bone, for a pilot who wears obsidian). A mix of one of
the two darkest shades is met in bone as well.

### Keeping the handheld's memory

A machine's pictures are coloured when a paint is first asked for and kept after. For the ready
paints that is a fixed number. For mixes it is not, so the pictures of a mix are forgotten again
once four other mixes have been asked for since.

## Approaches considered

For where a mix is kept: in the paint's own name, with the three kept names in the game (chosen:
whatever draws a machine needs to know nothing new); under a fixed name for each of the three,
looked up in the game by whatever draws (every drawing place would have to be handed the game, and
a changed mix would have to make pictures already made wrong); or as the full set of colour
numbers in the kept game (more to check when a game is opened, and nothing gained).

For where it is mixed: a third page of the garage's panel (chosen: the machine is there, and it is
changed the way paint is changed now); a room of its own in the hangar (the hangar's list is full
at eleven rows); or the Start button opening it from anywhere in the garage (a button the game has
never asked for, and one I cannot try on the handheld).

For what an untouched mix is: empty until first chosen, then a copy of the paint last worn (a pilot
only looking through the paints would fill all three without meaning to); or three ready-mixed ones
(chosen: there is no empty state to explain, and the first is the white machine the owner likes).

For how fine the steps are: sliders of a hundred steps (a long way to hold a button on a handheld,
and no two pilots' "red" would be the same); or few enough to count (chosen: twenty-four colours is
one press for a visible change, and a mix can be told to a friend).

## Constraints

- Every new text has its Dutch, inside the room its kind has. No pictures are made.
- No rule of combat and no number of the game's balance changes.
- A kept game from before the round opens as it was, and wears what it wore.
- Nothing is merged without the owner's word.

## Left to the builder

The exact values behind Finish, Shade and Glow, within what the ready paints already use; how the
strips are drawn and the chosen step marked; the words; whether there is a medal.
