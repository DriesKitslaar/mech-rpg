# Outstanding: the pilot who keeps coming back

Round after The Works. Stacked on `feat/works`.

## Intent, and why

The owner's two standing wishes are build variety and the feeling of power. The board has grown a
great deal (three contracts, a job of four kinds, a swarm, a contract that asks for a class), and all
of it is still made of strangers: nothing on it knows the pilot. The machines of the last rounds are
met in duels with named pilots, which is where the game is at its best, and there are only eight of
those.

This round gives the board one thing that is personal. A pilot the player has wrecked, driven off or
lost to comes back: by callsign, on a notice of their own beside the board, in their own machine,
rebuilt, and hardened against the weapon that hurt it most the last time. They come back three
times, and the third meeting closes the account. Then somebody else's begins.

It is for build variety because the machine remembers what it was hit with and says so: the weapon
that did most last time does half this time, the briefing names it and its odds show it, and the
hangar holds seven other builds. It is for power because the ones the pilot beats come back for
more, by name, having spent what they have on being ready for this one pilot.

## What was measured before anything was decided

Four ideas were flown by the game's own pilot before one was kept.

**The machine that got away.** One that breaks off when a pilot destroys two in a turn comes back.
Over forty-five boards (three contracts each, three flights):

| Board's tier | 0 to 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Won contracts in which a mech broke off | 0% | 4% | 7% | 0% | 23% | 19% |
| ... in which any fighting machine of two points or more did | 0 to 2% | 9% | 22% | 18% | 41% | 43% |

Below the fourth tier, which is the first twenty contracts and most of the story, nothing gets away.
A round built on that alone would be a round most pilots never meet.

**A machine rebuilt against the class the player flew**, with one of the four marks an elite can
carry. Each class's build for each stage against a named mech with each mark and the company the
budget leaves (wins in a hundred, the three stages together):

| Flown | No mark | Armoured | Overclocked | Swift | Shielded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Duellist | 97 | 91 | 92 | 92 | 55 |
| Marksman | 94 | 88 | 89 | 85 | 66 |
| Artillery | 86 | 76 | 82 | 79 | 22 |
| Lancer | 86 | 75 | 81 | 73 | 0 |
| Bastion | 91 | 81 | 86 | 82 | 7 |
| Skirmisher | 93 | 83 | 86 | 87 | 22 |
| All-rounder | 95 | 85 | 88 | 85 | 24 |

Three of the marks cost every class much the same five to ten wins: none is an answer to one class
and not another. The fourth shuts five of the seven out. There is no "built against your class" to
be made of marks.

**A machine hardened against every weapon it has been hurt by**, one more each meeting, three at the
most, and each time in a dearer machine. What tells builds apart is what they hit with, and over the
classes and the machines together this looked right (a build all of whose weapons were known still
won 53 to 76 times in a hundred). Taken machine by machine it was not. The third meeting on
Standard, the seven classes together, with every weapon of the build known:

| Board's tier | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mantis Ripper | 4 | 33 | 15 | 8 | 14 |
| Wraith Stalker | 7 | 35 | 33 | 39 | 26 |
| Halo Guard | 48 | 46 | 83 | 56 | 65 |
| Bulwark Enforcer | 100 | 96 | 86 | 96 | 100 |

A quick machine that takes half from everything is a wall: its fights were long already, and twice
as long is lost to the clock and to what it does in the meantime. A heavy one hardly notices. The
reader of this design found the same with flights of their own. Moving to "the next dearer machine"
made it worse, since four machines cost the same and two of them are these two.

**A machine hardened against one weapon** (kept): the one that did most to it the last time. Their
machine stays theirs, rebuilt a little thicker each time and no stronger. With the final numbers,
on Standard, tiers three to eight: over all machines and classes a build with nothing known wins 94
to 100 times in a hundred and one whose best weapon is known 84 to 98; machine by machine the
lowest are a Ripper's third meeting at the fourth tier (77 with nothing known, 50 with the best
weapon known) and a Stalker's at the eighth (84 and 65). The figures are in the plan.

## Approaches

1. **The one that got away, once.** True to what happened, but it does not happen before the late
   game, and one meeting is a hit with a name on it.
2. **The ambush.** The one who comes back joins a contract's battle uninvited. It has the most
   surprise in it. It also makes the briefing's odds a lie and punishes a build chosen for the
   contract. Not this.
3. **An account, answered with a mark for the class.** Measured: the marks do not tell the classes
   apart.
4. **An account, hardened against every weapon it has met, in dearer machines.** Measured: a wall
   where the machine is a quick one.
5. **An account, hardened against the one weapon that hurt it most the last time (chosen).** One
   named pilot at a time, begun by something that really happened in a contract: they beat the
   player, they broke off, or the player wrecked their machine and they walked out of it. They come
   back as a notice the player takes when they choose, three times, in the same machine. What is
   new is not one more fight but somebody who was there last time and remembers it.

## Goals

- From the board's third tier on (where its elites begin too), a pilot who plays the board meets an
  account within a handful of contracts, and one account is three meetings.
- Each meeting is one battle about one machine: the named pilot's own, with the company the board's
  budget leaves. It carries no mark. It is over when that machine falls.
- The machine is hardened against one weapon: the one that did most to it at the last meeting (when
  the account began, the one that did most in that contract). A weapon it is hardened against does
  half to it, and all it ever did to anything else on the field. The briefing names the weapon, and
  the figure shown while aiming is the halved one.
- A build that brings that weapon again is at a disadvantage and not shut out: flown in each class's
  build for the stage, the game's own pilot wins the meeting at least about as often as not on
  Standard against every machine an account can be about, the classes together. A build that does
  not bring it meets the machine as it would any named machine of the board.
- The pilot speaks: as the fight opens (by how the account began, on the first meeting), and when
  their machine falls. The third fall closes the account in their own words.
- The pilot's page counts accounts closed; three medals; a chapter of the manual and of the guide;
  everything in English and Dutch.

## Non-goals

- No new machines, parts or pictures of machines. One rule of battle is new: a machine can be
  hardened against weapons, by their ids, and those do half to it.
- The account does not begin in the arena, a duel, the simulator, the range or the Gauntlet, and
  never with a machine that belongs to the story.
- No faces drawn for these pilots this round: they are a callsign and a voice.
- No list of accounts in the database.

## Anti-goals

- A notice that cannot be left alone, or anything that follows the pilot into another contract.
- A build that cannot win because it was the build that won last time.
- Any change to the board's three contracts, the job or the swarm: each stays, for the same
  progress, exactly what it was. The notice is on dice of its own.
- A callsign that is already somebody's name in the game.
- A game saved before this round that does not load, or loads with an account it never had.

## How it works

### The account

Kept in the saved game, absent in a game that has none:

- `name`: the callsign, one of sixteen, chosen by the pilot's progress; a name already closed is not
  used again while any is left.
- `kind`: their machine: a mech of the board's pool. It does not change.
- `origin`: `beat` (they won, or the player retreated, with their machine standing), `ran` (their
  machine broke off) or `wrecked` (the player destroyed it).
- `knows`: the weapon their machine is hardened against, by its id; absent where the player has hit
  them with nothing yet.
- `met`: meetings the player has won: 0, 1 or 2.
- `waited`: contracts won since the account began or since the last meeting.

And `accounts`: the callsigns of the accounts closed, in order.

### How one begins

When a contract is booked (won or lost), no account is open and the board is at its third tier or
beyond, the contract's battles are read for a mech of the board's pool: one standing when the player
lost or retreated, else one that broke off under its own power, else one that was destroyed. The
strongest of those reasons wins, and among machines with the same reason the dearest, the first of
them when two cost the same. Contracts that count for nothing (the simulator, the range, the
Gauntlet) and fights in a lent mech begin none. What the account's pilot knows from there is the
weapon of the player's that took the most armour off the other side in that contract (none, if the
player hit nothing).

### When they come back

The notice is on offer once `waited` reaches 3 for the first meeting and 2 for the second and third.
It is an open contract with an id of its own (`open-<cycle>-<battlesWon>-grudge`), listed after the
job and before the swarm. Like everything beside the board it is a pure function of the saved game:
the same after a reload, made again after every contract won. It stays until it is won; losing it
changes nothing.

### The meeting

One battle: the named machine first, then the company the board's budget for the tier leaves after
its cost (nothing that mends; none at all if nothing is left: then it came alone). The objective
names the machine, as a hit's does, so the battle is fought by the rules of a fight about one
machine: no momentum, no shatter, nobody breaks. The machine is hardened against `knows` and plated
a little thicker at the second meeting and thicker again at the third; the enemy scale is the
board's own for the tier. The fee is a hit's fee for that machine times a factor for the meeting.
The plating and the factors are set by measurement in the first task.

Won: `met` goes up, `waited` starts again, and `knows` becomes the weapon that took the most armour
off the named machine in this meeting (it stays what it was if nothing of the player's hit it). The
third win removes the account and writes the callsign under `accounts`.

### The words

Dispatch briefs it. Three paragraphs: the place; who this is and how it began (the first meeting, by
origin) or which meeting this is (the second and third); and which weapon the machine is hardened
against. In battle the pilot has a line as it opens and one as their machine falls, Dispatch one
when the player's core is below half. A machine that is hardened says so over its head as an elite
says its mark, and the weapon in hand says so while it is aimed at that machine. The bill of a
meeting says which of the three it was; the bill of the third says the account is paid in full.

## Constraints

- The board, the job and the swarm are generated exactly as before for every progress.
- No balance test of what exists changes.
- Every new text has its Dutch, within the room its kind has; interface strings in Dutch at most two
  letters longer than the English, with the same numbers in the same order.
- A briefing's body, with a line under it for what the contract asks and one for the ground, is at
  most eight lines.
- The proxy's address is never written into the repository. No pictures are needed for this round.

## Left to the builder

- The sixteen callsigns (none may be a name the game already uses) and the exact lines.
- Where on the bill and the pilot's page the new lines go.
- The names of the three medals and what each is for (a first meeting won, an account closed, three
  closed).
- How the measurements are run, as long as the figures that set the numbers are written into the
  plan.
