# The Masters

## Why

Three rounds have made a pilot's builds matter: seven classes of machine, the build chosen at the
briefing with its odds beside it, a contract that pays more for a class, a range to try a build on
and the pilot's own saved builds to spar with. What the late game still lacks is a goal for each
class. The arena's ladder is one ladder for any build; the duels at the end of the story are two
fights for any build. Nothing asks a pilot to build a bastion and win as one, and nothing says so
when they have.

The standing brief is to keep working, on my own judgment, towards a game that plays like a
finished one; the user's last word on what matters was variety in builds, and before that the
feeling of power. This round gives each class a summit, and a title to take from the one standing
on it.

## Goals

1. **The Masters.** A second page of the arena: seven pilots, each the master of one class, each
   in the purest machine of that class. They take challenges from the champion of the ladder. Any
   build may challenge, for a purse. A pilot who beats a master **in a machine of the master's own
   class** takes that class's **crown**.
2. **What a crown gives.** It is seen: on the pilot's page of classes, and in a paint that only
   its holder may wear. It pays: a contract that asks for that class pays its
   holder twice the usual bonus. Two medals count them.
3. An APK after every step. No rule of combat changes, and no balance test of what exists changes.

## Not goals

- No new parts, frames or weapons, and no new pictures: a master's machine is put together from
  the parts the game has, as the ladder's are.
- No bonus in combat for holding a crown. The bench and the perks are where a pilot grows stronger;
  a crown that made its class hit harder would have every fight in the game measured again.
- The ladder, the back room, the mirror match and the cups stay as they are.

## What would make it a failure

- A crown had by flying another class's machine at a master, or had twice, or lost.
- A master that an honest late build of its own class cannot beat, or one that any such build
  beats without trying.
- A duel that drags: twenty rounds of two machines that cannot hurt each other.
- A page whose rows or words do not fit, in either language.
- A kept game from before this round that does not load, or loads with a crown it did not win.

## How it works

### The page and the duel (K1)

The arena has two pages, turned with L1 and R1 as the simulator's are: LADDER, as it was, and
MASTERS. The masters' page lists the seven classes in the order the rules ask about them. Beside
the list stands the master of the class under the cursor in their own paint; under it their name,
which class they are the master of, how they fight, and how it stands between them and the pilot:
locked, open, beaten, or the crown held.

The page is there from the start and locked until the pilot is champion of the ladder (rank ten):
the masters, like the back room, are for a pilot who has finished the climb.

A opens a briefing like any arena fight: how the master fights, what they say, the build row and
the odds. One line more says what the crown asks: a win in a machine of the master's class. It is
lit when the build chosen is of that class, and points to the saved build that is when another is
chosen, as the line of a contract that asks for a class does. No wingman comes: it is one against
one.

The duel is one battle in the arena. The master's machine is of their class by the same rule that
names the pilot's, and it is flown as the game flies that class when a pilot spars with a saved
build: a duellist closes in, a skirmisher darts, artillery spreads its fire. So a pilot can practise
on the range against the kind of flying they will meet.

Beating a master pays a purse of 9000 the first time and a quarter of it after, as the mirror match
and the cups do, whatever was flown. Beating a master in a machine of their class also takes the
crown, once and for good: the settlement says so, the master has a word about it in place of the
usual debrief, and a notice comes up as for a medal. A master who has lost the crown still takes
challenges.

### How strong

Each master is set by measurement, as the ladder is. The measure is the late build the game
suggests for the master's own class, flown on Auto Turn. With the perks of a pilot who has finished
the story (six ranks) it should take the crown about four or five times in ten; with no perks,
seldom; with every perk there is, most of the time and not every time.

Two figures set a master: what the duel scales them by (armour and damage alike) and plating
(armour alone). A class that kills quickly meets a master who is plated to last and hits no harder
for it; a class that is slow to kill meets one who hits harder and is plated little, so that the
duel is decided before the magazines are empty. The figures are found in K1 and held by tests as
ranges, not to the percent: twenty flights are too few for more.

A machine of another class may well find a master easier than their own class does: energy weapons
go through an artillery frame. That is the purse. The crown is the harder thing by design.

### What a crown gives (K2)

- **The title.** The pilot's page of classes marks each class whose crown is held, counts them,
  and calls such a class by what its holder is called. (The record was to have a row for them;
  its two columns are full, so the count stands on the page of classes.)
- **The master's paint.** Seven schemes, one a master. The garage offers each only to the pilot
  who holds that crown.
- **Pay.** A contract on the board that asks for a class pays a quarter more to a pilot who wins
  it as that class. To the holder of that class's crown it pays half as much again instead. The
  briefing and the settlement say the figure that applies.
- **Two medals:** for the first crown, and for all seven.

## Approaches considered

For where the masters are: more rungs on the ladder (one ladder for any build says nothing about
classes), an entry of their own in the hub (the hub has eleven rows and no room), or a second page
of the arena (chosen): it is where one fights one for a purse, and a page is a pattern the
simulator already has.

For what a master asks: only a machine of the class may challenge (a pilot without one is turned
away from a fight they could have had, and the page has to explain why), or any machine may, and
only the class takes the crown (chosen): two goals in one fight, nobody turned away, and the
briefing's build row already shows which saved build is of the class.

For what a crown gives: a bonus in combat for the class (every fight measured again; the bench
does this already), a perk point (there are nineteen points for twenty-three ranks, so three of
seven crowns would give nothing), or a title, a paint, pay and medals (chosen): seen in every
fight, and nothing in combat changes.

For how a master flies: a boss with phases and tricks of its own (new machinery, and nothing learnt
about the class), or the flying the game already gives each class (chosen): what a master does is
what the class does, which is the point of them.

## Constraints

- A kept game loads and is as it was. Crowns and masters beaten are kept like the cups: they stay
  when the story starts over.
- Every new English text has its Dutch, inside the room its kind has.
- Nothing is merged without the user's word.
- No pictures are bought: the machines are composed of parts that have theirs, and paint is
  arithmetic.

## Left to the builder

The masters' names and words, their machines within their class, the figures within the measure
above, the colours of the seven paints, where things stand on the page, and the names of the medals.
