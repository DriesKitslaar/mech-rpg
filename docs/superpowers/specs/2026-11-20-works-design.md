# The Works

## Why

The owner asked for machines for the end of the game that a pilot wants for what they are: fast,
elegant, a little frightening. Two exist that are bought after a duel (the Sterling and the Shrike)
and one for the very end (the Sovereign). Each has one thing no other frame has, and that is what
makes it wanted.

The round before this one added five kinds of weapon that ask something of the machine that carries
them. Two of them ask for a machine that does not exist. A redline hits harder the hotter its
machine runs, and every frame there is was built to keep its heat down. A surge takes all the
energy that is left, and in every frame there is what is left at the end of a turn is gone.

This round adds the two machines those weapons were waiting for: one that runs hot, and one that
keeps what it does not spend. The yard on the coast that builds the Circuit's machines has two it
has never sold. It sends its own works pilots to Varn in them, and whoever beats one may order one.

## Goals

1. **Two new frames for the end of the game**, designs of this game's own, each with one thing no
   other frame has, each a different machine to fly:
   - **Sirocco** runs hot. Its heat limit is 130 where every other machine's is 100.
   - **Solstice** keeps what it does not spend. Energy left at the end of its turn is carried into
     its next, up to half of what a turn gives it.
2. **Two duels**, posted one at a time once the Circuit's first sword has fallen. The pilot meets
   each frame on the field before owning it, as with the first two.
3. **Each works pilot fights with what the machine is for**, so that what the frame does is learnt
   from the other end of it: one hits harder every round it is left alone, the other holds a round
   and then spends two rounds' energy at once.
4. **Seen where the pilot decides**: the heat bar is drawn to the machine's own limit, what was
   kept is said as the turn opens, and the core's line in the shop and the garage says what it does.
5. **Flown properly by the game itself** (Auto Turn, the odds, a sparring partner, the mirror).
6. An APK after every step.

## Not goals

- No new class of machine, and nothing about how a build is classed.
- No change to a frame, a weapon or a fight that exists.
- No change to what an overheat is or does: only to where one machine's limit lies.
- No third thing: the head, the arms and the legs of each frame are figures and nothing more.
- No new story beyond the two duels, their pilots' pages in the database and what the yard writes.

## What would make it a failure

- A frame that makes the audit's verdict, the masters or the eighth seat a formality.
- A frame that is simply better: each must lose to a frame that exists in some kind of fight, and
  be no better than one with weapons that are not of its kinds.
- A limit that cannot be read. A pilot in a Sirocco who is warned at 100, or one in anything else
  who is not, has been lied to by the screen.
- A duel that is over in two rounds, or one that cannot be won without the counter the briefing names.
- Machines that look like the ones there are with other paint.

## The two frames

### Sirocco

*It runs hot.*

A long-limbed machine in soot black and burnt orange, with a mane of heat-sink fins down its back
that glow when it is warm, and a narrow slit for a visor. A design of this game's own.

Its core has a heat limit of 130. Everything that asks where the limit is asks the machine: whether
a shot overheats it, how far a redline's heat counts, when the game's own pilot vents or holds a
weapon back, how long the heat bar is and when it turns red, when the machine glows on the field,
and what the weapon's row warns of. An overheat is what it always was: the next turn is lost and
the heat comes down to fifty.

What it is for: a volley with one weapon more in it, and a redline that climbs thirty further than
in any other machine. What it costs: it is plated lightly for its weight, and its heat still has to
go somewhere: the generator cools it as it would any machine.

### Solstice

*It keeps what it does not spend.*

A tall, narrow machine in indigo and silver-white with thin cyan seams, and two long capacitor
blades that rise from its back like a tuning fork. A design of this game's own.

Its core has a buffer. Whatever energy the machine has left when its turn ends is kept, up to half
of what a turn gives it, and is there on top of the next turn's energy. (The word on the screen is
BUFFER in both languages: RESERVE is what the Dutch calls one of the Gauntlet's relics.) A turn that is lost (to
heat, to a stagger) loses nothing that was kept. What comes off a turn's energy (a drain, a
stagger's half) comes off the turn's own share, as ever, and not off what was kept.

What it is for: a held round and then a surge with half as much again behind it; a guard and a
full volley in the same turn; nothing wasted in a turn that was not all needed. What it costs: it
is plated lightly, and a round held is a round in which nothing was fired.

### How a pilot comes by them

Two more duels in the series there is (`duel-3`, `duel-4`), each on offer once the one before it is
won. The yard's works pilots: **Kiln** in the Sirocco, **Vesper** in the Solstice. A duel won opens
that frame's four parts in the shop (`won:duel-3`, `won:duel-4`), for good, as the first two do.

### The two works pilots

- **Kiln** carries a redline in each hand and nothing that cools. Left alone the Sirocco is hotter
  every round and hits harder for it. The briefing says what to do about that: knock it off balance
  (a staggered machine has half its energy and fires less), or heat it past even its own limit so
  that it loses a turn.
- **Vesper** carries a surge. The Solstice fires nothing on every other round: it guards, keeps its
  energy, and says so in what it is about to do, where an enemy's next move is always shown. The
  round after, the surge has a turn and a half behind it. The briefing says what to do: hit it in
  the round it holds, guard in the round it fires, and a weapon that drains takes what it kept.

Both are plated to last a duel, as the first two swords are.

## Where it is seen

- **The core's line** in the shop and the garage: what the limit is, what the buffer keeps.
- **The heat bar** of the pilot's own machine is as long as its limit, and its figure says the
  limit where it is not the usual one. The weapon's row and the aiming line warn of an overheat at
  the machine's own limit.
- **What was kept** is said as the turn opens: a word over the machine, and the energy bar's figure
  is more than a turn gives.
- **A works pilot's next move** says when the Solstice is holding.
- **The manual** gets a chapter, the guide and the README a section, the database a page for each
  pilot and a note on each machine. One medal, for beating both.

## Measuring

As the balance of everything else was measured: the game's own pilot, a hundred flights a contract,
in builds a pilot at the end of the story could own.

- With the weapons it is for, each frame wins more often than the best frame there was with the
  same weapons, in the fights those weapons are for.
- With weapons that are not of its kinds, it wins no more often than the best frame there was.
- Neither takes the eighth seat, the audit's verdict in a later telling, or the hardest master more
  than a few points in a hundred more often than the best build there was.
- Each duel is won about half the time by a good build that ignores the briefing, and most of the
  time by one that follows it.

## Approaches considered

For what the hot machine does: a higher limit (chosen: one figure, read everywhere, and the game's
own pilot uses it without being taught anything); no lost turn but armour burnt for every point
past the limit (a decision every turn, but one the game's own pilot could not make well, so the
odds at a briefing would be wrong for it); every weapon hitting harder with heat (that is a redline
again, on everything).

For what the other does: energy carried over (chosen: it changes what a turn is, and a surge makes
it visible); a larger cell (only a better generator); a stance that costs nothing (strong, and
nothing to do with the weapons the round is about).

For how they are earned: two more duels (chosen: the series is there, a pilot meets the frame
before owning it, and a works pilot can show what the frame is for); ranks of the arena (the arena
has the masters and the seat already); the shop outright (nothing earned).

For the works pilots: the frame with ordinary weapons (cheap; the duel would teach nothing); or
weapons and a habit that show the frame's one thing (chosen).

## Constraints

- A kept game from before the round opens as it was; a battle is never kept, so what a unit carries
  through a battle may grow.
- Every new text has its Dutch, inside the room its kind has.
- Pictures at the lowest quality: two frames from the front and from behind, two faces, two places.
  Well under a dollar; the whole budget stands at $7.26 of $14.
- Inspiration in tone only: no machine, name, mark or colour scheme of anybody else's game.
- Nothing is merged without the owner's word.

## Left to the builder

Every figure; the names of parts and weapons; the words; what the two places are; how the bar says
a limit that is not the usual one; how the kept energy is drawn; the medal.
