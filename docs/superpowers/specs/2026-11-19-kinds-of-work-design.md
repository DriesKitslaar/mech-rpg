# Kinds of Work

## Why

The last five rounds were about builds: seven classes, a build chosen at the briefing, the odds
for it, a range to try it on, masters to beat as each class, and seven places to keep one of each.
What a pilot flies those builds on has not changed. The board, where a finished pilot does most of
their flying, offers three contracts and a swarm, and every one of them asks the same thing:
destroy everything on the field. A contract that asks for a class pays more for it, but nothing
about the fight makes that class the one to bring.

The rules already know five other things a battle can ask for (the story uses them: a machine to
kill and no other, a machine to take in one piece, freight to keep alive, a number of rounds to
last). The board uses none.

The standing brief is to keep working, on my own judgment, towards a game that plays like a
finished one; the owner's word on what matters was variety in builds.

## Goals

1. **A fourth open contract on the board that asks for something else.** One of four kinds, a new
   one after every battle won, turning through all four:
   - **Hit**: one machine is named and paid for; when it falls the job is over. It is an elite.
   - **Repossession**: one machine is wanted back in one piece: its arms and legs gone, its core
     whole. Destroy it and the job is lost.
   - **Escort**: the client's freight is worn down every round by whatever still stands. Lost
     when the freight is.
   - **Holding action**: more than the fee could ask anyone to beat, and more behind it. Last six
     rounds.
2. **The kind of work decides which build is the one to bring**, and the odds at the briefing show
   it: measured, the classes are not all alike on any of the four, and on at least two of them the
   best and the worst class are far apart.
3. Each kind says what it is where a pilot chooses: in the list by its title, in the briefing by
   its heading, its words and the objective line the story's contracts already have.
4. Two medals, for a pilot who does the work: one of each kind, and many of them.
5. An APK as soon as the jobs can be played, and at the end. No rule of combat changes.

## Not goals

- No change to the board's own three contracts, to the swarm, or to the patrol: every board is
  otherwise the board it was.
- No new rule in a battle: every kind is made from objectives, marks, reserves and a scale the
  rules already have.
- No new art.

## What would make it a failure

- A job that cannot be won as some class at the stage it is offered, or one no class can lose.
- A job the rules refuse (a hit with one machine on the field, a repossession with nothing that has
  arms and legs), on any board a pilot can come to.
- A kind that pays so much better for the time it takes that the rest of the board is not worth
  flying, or so much worse that it is not.
- A title, heading or line that does not fit, in either language.

## How it works

Beside the board's three and the swarm stands one more open contract, from the board's first tier
on (five battles won). Like them it is a pure function of the pilot's progress, on dice of its
own: the same job after a reload, a new one after every battle won. Its kind is not on the dice:
the four turn in order, so that four battles won have shown all four.

Each is one battle, at the board's own tier.

| kind | on the field | asks | made harder by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hit | the board's roll, never fewer than two | the dearest machine destroyed | that machine carries an elite's mark; the job has a scale of its own |
| Repossession | a mech, and what is left of the budget in machines that are not | its arms and legs gone, its core whole | nothing: the care it takes is the difficulty |
| Escort | the board's roll | all of them destroyed before the freight is gone | how little the freight can take |
| Holding action | a roll on a larger budget, and a reserve behind it | six rounds outlasted | a scale of its own |

The numbers (scales, what the freight takes, the reserve, the pay) are set by measurement: every
class's build for the stage, flown by the game's own pilot on both difficulties, as the masters
were set. The targets: no class under one win in three on Standard at the stage it meets the job,
no kind on which every class wins nine in ten on Veteran, and pay for a round of fighting within
half as much again of the board's own contracts.

A job is told from the rest of the board by its title, which begins with its kind (`Hit:`,
`Repo:`, `Escort:`, `Hold:`) as a wanted notice begins with `Wanted:`. Its briefing is headed
`OPEN CONTRACT: A HIT` and so on, says in a sentence or two what the kind asks, and carries the
objective line the story's contracts have. What is asked is on the field as it is there: the
marked machine, the freight's meter, the count of rounds.

A won job is counted by its kind in the kept game. Two medals read the count.

## Approaches considered

For where the kinds go: laid over the board's own three (one contract of the three becomes a
job: no longer list, but every board a pilot has seen changes, and with it every test and picture
that flies one), or a row of their own beside the swarm (chosen: the board is the board it was,
and there is always one job on offer).

For which kind is on offer: on the dice (the same kind can come up four times running), or in
turn (chosen).

For how a job is told in the list: a mark beside the pay as a class asked for has (says that it is
special, not what it is), or its kind at the head of its title (chosen: the list has room for
eighteen letters, and wanted notices already do it).

## Constraints

- A kept game loads and is as it was; the count of jobs is a field a kept game may lack.
- Every new English text has its Dutch, inside the room its kind has.
- Nothing is merged without the owner's word.

## Left to the builder

The numbers, by measurement; the titles and the words; where the count is shown.
