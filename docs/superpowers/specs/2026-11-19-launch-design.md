# Launch

## Why

A contract is taken at a desk and begins on a field. Between the two the game shows a pair of doors
and a banner: the machine the pilot spent an hour building is first seen when the shooting has
already started. Every game about machines like these has the moment in between, and it is the one
that says the machine is the pilot's: it stands on the catapult, the lamps count down, and it goes.

The owner asked for a feeling of power above all, and the standing brief is to keep working on my
own judgment towards a game that plays like a finished one. This round gives every contract its
launch. It is short, it is seen from the first contract on, and one press skips it.

## Goals

1. **The launch.** When a contract flown in the pilot's own machine begins, the doors open on a
   launch tunnel: the machine from behind, in its paint, on the catapult. Three lamps come on one
   after another, the catapult fires, the machine goes down the tunnel into the light, and the
   battle opens out of that light with its banner as before.
2. **Short, and the player's to cut.** About a second and a half. Any of A or B ends it at once. A
   player who has asked for faster battles does not see it at all.
3. **The machine's own.** It is the build that is flown, in the paint it wears; its class and the
   pilot's callsign are written over it; a heavy machine goes with a longer rumble than a light one.
4. An APK after every step.

## Not goals

- No launch for a fight that does not start from the hangar: the arena, the simulator's drills and
  range, a floor of the Gauntlet. Their openings stay as they are.
- Nothing after the first battle of a contract: the later ones follow on the field.
- No rule of combat, no number, no kept game changes. Nothing new is stored.
- No new option on the options page: faster battles already says the player wants less to watch.
- No wingman in the tunnel, and no second picture for a second place.

## What would make it a failure

- A launch that cannot be cut short, or that takes a press meant for the battle.
- A battle that starts while the launch is still on the screen: a turn taken unseen.
- A launch shown in a hurried game (the tests' own), so that every test of a contract waits for it.
- A flash in a calm picture. A picture that shakes there.
- A machine drawn in another build or paint than the one that is flown.
- Words that do not fit, in either language.
- A launch that makes the handheld drop frames: it is drawn with what a battle is drawn with.

## How it works

### What is seen

One picture of a launch tunnel, looked down from its back wall: strip lights along walls and
ceiling that meet at a bright opening at the far end, a catapult rail down the middle of the floor.
The machine stands on the rail at the bottom of the picture, seen from behind as it is on the
field, under the opening.

- **Ready** (the first 700 ms; the doors are still opening for the first 320 of them): three lamps
  at the top of the picture come on in turn, each with a short tone. On the left stands the
  pilot's callsign with the class of the build under it; on the right the word for where this is.
- **Launch** (the 800 ms after): the catapult fires. A word in the logo's letters stands across the
  picture for a moment. The machine goes down the rail towards the opening, smaller as it goes,
  with the glow of its boosters under it; lines of light run outwards from the opening; the picture
  shakes and the handheld rumbles, longer for a heavy machine. The opening blooms until the picture
  is white.
- **Out of the light**: the battle is drawn under that white as it fades (200 ms), and its banner
  begins. The battle does not move until then.

In a calm picture nothing shakes, the lines of light are few and still, and the picture goes to the
battle through dark and not through white.

### When it is shown

For the first battle of a contract that is flown in the pilot's own machine and is no arena fight:
the story's contracts, the board's, the jobs, the swarms, the other side's offers, the two duels
after the story, and contracts flown again from the archive. Not in a hurried game, and not for a
player who has turned faster battles on.

### Cutting it short

A or B ends the launch at once: the battle is there, with its banner beginning, and no white over
it. The press is used up by that: it does not also dismiss the banner or pick a row.

## Approaches considered

For the place of it: a screen of its own between briefing and battle (every test and helper that
waits for the battle would have to learn a new screen, and the battle's music would have to be
started from two places), or a first stage of the contract's own screen (chosen: the screen stays
the battle, which simply has not begun to move; whatever waits for the battle already waits).

For the picture: drawn by the program from lines (exactly where the program wants it, and plainer
than every other place in the game), or one picture from the art pipeline with the moving light
laid over it by the program (chosen: it looks like the rest of the game, and costs one picture).

For how often: every contract (chosen, with the cut and with faster battles), only the story's
contracts (the board's are most of the game, and would be the poorer), or once a sitting (a player
would take it for a fault that it stopped).

## Constraints

- One picture at the service's lowest quality, inside the budget the ledger keeps.
- Every new text has its Dutch, inside the room its kind has.
- Drawn inside what a frame of battle costs: one picture, one machine, a few dozen rectangles.
- Nothing is merged without the owner's word.

## Left to the builder

The words; the exact times; where the lamps and the words stand; the colour of the light; how the
rumble follows the weight; the sounds.
