# The Range

## Why

The last two rounds made a pilot's builds matter: seven classes, two frames to win at the end, the
build chosen at the briefing, the odds beside it, a contract that pays more for a class. What the
game still lacks is a place to try a build. Every flight that is not a lesson bills ammunition and
repairs, and a lost one bills more; the simulator only lends machines for its drills. A pilot who
has just bought a blade wants to swing it at something they know, now, for nothing, and to see
what it would have cost.

The standing brief is to keep working, on my own judgment, towards a game that plays like a
finished one; the user's last word on what matters was variety in builds. This round gives that
variety a workshop floor.

## Goals

1. **The range.** A second page of the simulator. Every kind of machine the pilot has met, one to
   three of them, fought in the pilot's own build. Nothing is billed, paid or counted; the bill a
   real flight would have had is shown.
2. **Sparring.** On the same page, the pilot's saved builds as opponents.
3. An APK after every step. No rule of combat changes, and no balance changes.

## Not goals

- No rewards of any kind: no pay, experience, medals, ranks, wins for a class, or records.
- No new machines, and no editing of a build from the range: the garage is where builds are made.

## What would make it a failure

- A flight on the range that changes anything kept, other than fitting the build chosen at its
  briefing (as accepting any contract does).
- A fight on the range that is not the real thing: without the pilot's perks, the bench's work,
  the Overdrive or the difficulty set.
- A list that runs out of its panel, or a text that does not fit, in either language.

## How it works

### The page (P1)

The simulator has two pages, turned with L1 and R1 as the pilot's screen is: DRILLS, as it was,
and RANGE. The range lists every kind of machine in the database that the pilot has met, in the
database's order. Left and right on a row set how many are fielded: up to three of the machines an
open contract fields in numbers, one of a mech and of anything that belongs to the story. Beside
the list stands the machine; under it, what the database says of it.

A opens a briefing like any other: the build row, and the odds, work as they do for a contract.
The contract is a fight in the simulator's hall against what was chosen, as the book has the
machines and as hard as the game is set.

The fight is a real one in every rule: the pilot's own build and paint, perks, the bench's work
and the Overdrive. Like a drill it has the simulator's voice only: Dispatch's once-only lessons
are not spent there, and no name is flown under.

When it ends, the bill is shown as a real flight would have had it: ammunition fired and armour
lost, part by part. It is marked as not billed, and nothing of it is booked: credits, experience,
contracts won, wins by class, the record, medals and the machines met all stay as they were.

### Sparring (P2)

Above the machines the range lists the pilot's saved builds that can be flown, as opponents: a
machine built from those parts, with the bench's work on them, flown by the game. Which of two
builds beats the other is then a thing a pilot can find out, and the odds say it beforehand.

## Approaches considered

For where a build is tried: a button in the garage (the garage has no button left, and no room
for a list of opponents), a page of the arena (the arena is where fights count), or a page of the
simulator (chosen): it is already the place where nothing is billed, and it has the room.

For what is fought: a fixed set of dummies (says little about the machines a pilot actually has
trouble with), any machine in the game (spoils what has not been met), or every machine met
(chosen), which also makes the range a place to practise a boss that was barely beaten.

For what follows a fight: straight back to the list (quickest), or the bill it would have had
(chosen): what a build costs to fly is half of what a pilot wants to know about it.

## Constraints

- A kept game loads and is as it was.
- Every new English text has its Dutch, inside the room its kind has.
- Nothing is merged without the user's word.

## Left to the builder

The words, where things stand on the page, the simulator's song for it, and how a saved build is
flown when it is the opponent.
