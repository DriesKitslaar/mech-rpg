# Freight on the Field

## Why

The last round put escorts on the board: a job that is lost when the client's freight is. It is
the kind on which the class flown matters most, and the one that looks least like what it is. A
hit has its marked machine on the field, a repossession its wanted one, a holding action its
count of rounds and the machines that keep coming. An escort has a name and a meter in the corner:
`WAGE TRUCK 11/11`. What is being fought over is nowhere to be seen. The story's own escorts (an
ore train, a stack of cores) have always been the same.

The standing brief is to keep working, on my own judgment, towards a game that plays like a
finished one.

## Goals

1. **What an escort keeps alive stands on the field**: a picture of its own for each of the four
   things a job can ask a pilot to guard (a wage truck, an ore lift, a motorcade, a pump barge),
   for the two the story has that are things and not places (the ore train, the core stack), and
   for the convoy of a drill and a favour.
2. **Its state is seen on it.** It is struck when the round begins and the machines against it
   take their piece: it shakes, it flashes, and what it lost is written at it. Worn down to a
   third it smokes. Lost, it comes apart.
3. Nothing about the rules of an escort changes: what is drawn is what the meter already says.
4. An APK when the freight can be seen, and at the end.

## Not goals

- No picture for the hangar the story has a pilot defend: that fight is inside it, and the place
  is the picture. None for a wingman's frame either, which would be a mech and not a thing.
- Nothing aims at the freight and nothing can be done to it by the pilot: it is not a unit.
- No change to the strip that names it and counts it down.

## What would make it a failure

- Freight that hides a machine, or the pilot's own, or is hidden by them so that it cannot be made
  out, in any line-up the board or the story fields against it.
- A picture that reads as one of the enemy's machines.
- A battle without freight that looks any different from before.
- Anything that moves in a calm picture, or anything a hurried screen waits for.

## How it works

A battle that asks for something protected may name a picture for it. The battle carries the
picture's name with the freight's name and what it can take; a battle that names none (the
hangar; any kept game or test that makes its own) is drawn as it always was.

The field is looked at over the pilot's shoulder: the pilot's mech near and to the left, what it
fights further off to the right. The freight stands between them, on the far line of the floor,
left of where the first of the enemy stands: ahead of the pilot and short of the machines that are
taking it apart. It is drawn before them and before the pilot's mech, so that whatever stands
nearer stands in front of it, with a shadow under it like everything else on the floor.

When a round begins and the freight loses something, the picture is thrown a little and lit white
for a moment, sparks come off it, and the figure it lost is written over it. From a third of what
it could take downwards, smoke rises from it. When nothing is left it comes apart as a machine
that is one picture does, and the battle is over. In a calm picture it does not shake and is not
lit; a hurried screen draws it standing, as it draws everything.

The pictures are made as every machine's was: drawn from the side by the image service against a
frame of the game as the reference for colour and line, cut out, and brought to the game's size
and palette. They are in two liveries no enemy machine wears (the bone white of the Courier for
what belongs to the city, the rust red of the Dozer for what belongs to the pits), so that freight
is not taken for a target.

## Approaches considered

For where it is: a small framed picture by the strip that names it (always in view, but a badge
and not a thing on the field), behind the pilot where the story would have it (out of the
picture: the camera looks the other way), or on the floor between the pilot and the enemy
(chosen: it is what the fight is over, and there is room for it left of the enemy's line).

For how a battle knows the picture: by the freight's name (which is translated, so the name on a
Dutch field would find nothing), or a picture named beside it (chosen).

## Constraints

- Seven pictures at the service's lowest quality, inside the budget the ledger keeps (they came to thirteen cents).
- A kept game loads and is as it was. Nothing is merged without the owner's word.

## Left to the builder

Exactly where it stands and how large, by looking at it in every place it can be; how hard it
shakes; the words of the manual and the guide.
