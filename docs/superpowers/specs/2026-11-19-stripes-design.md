# Stripes

## Why

A build is one of seven classes, and the game counts what a pilot has won as each. Nothing comes
of the count but three medals and, at the very top, the crowns. A pilot who finds one build that
works has no reason to put another together: the hangar holds seven builds and most players will
fly one.

The owner said variety of builds and the classes matter most, next to a feeling of power. The
standing brief is to keep working on my own judgment towards a game that plays like a finished
one. This round makes flying another class pay, in a way that is kept: what was won as each
class counts towards perk points, and the first of them comes soonest to a pilot who flies many
kinds of machine.

## Goals

1. **Stripes.** A class gives the pilot a stripe at 3, at 8 and at 15 contracts won flying it.
   Three a class, twenty-one in all.
2. **Every fifth stripe is a perk point.** Of whatever classes: five stripes held is a point, ten is
   two, and so to four at twenty. The cheapest stripes are the first of each class, so the first
   point is had soonest by flying five kinds of machine.
3. **Seen where classes and skills are seen, and where a build is chosen.** The pilot's page of
   classes shows each class's stripes, how many are held in all and when the next point comes;
   the page of perks has the point to spend; a briefing says of the build a contract will be
   flown in how near its class is to its next stripe; a stripe is announced as it is earned, and
   says so when it brought a point.
4. An APK after every step.

## Not goals

- No new rule of combat and no new kind of bonus. A stripe gives nothing in a fight by itself.
- No stripe for the simulator, a lent machine or the Gauntlet's own frame: they are nobody's class.
- Nothing is taken away: a stripe once earned is kept, through every telling, like a perk.
- No balance test of what exists changes, and no fight is flown by a stronger pilot than the game
  is measured for: the most a pilot can come to hold is every perk, as before.
- No new page and no new option.

## What would make it a failure

- A reward that makes one class better only at being itself: that would pay for flying one build
  for ever, which is the opposite of what this is for.
- A pilot stronger than the game is measured for (see below, what was tried first).
- A kept game from before this round that loads with stripes it should not have, or without the
  ones its record has earned: the record is what counts, and nothing new is stored.
- A point that can be spent twice, or that is lost: the points are counted from the record every
  time, with the levels', and what is spent is what the perks hold.
- A page whose rows or words do not fit, in either language.

## How it works

### Earning them

The pilot's record already says how many contracts were won as each class (the WON column of the
classes page). Stripes are read off it: one at 3, two at 8, three at 15. Nothing new is kept, so a
game from before this round has at once the stripes its record has earned and the points they
bring. Nothing announces those: the hangar marks points to spend, as it does after a level, and
the classes page shows the stripes.

### What they bring

One perk point for every five stripes held, up to four. A pilot at the highest level has nineteen
points from levels, and the perks have twenty-three ranks between them: with twenty stripes a
pilot can at last hold every rank of every perk, and has nothing left over. The twenty-first
stripe brings a medal.

### What was tried first, and why it was dropped

The first design had each class train one skill in every machine (the Sharpshooter's stripes
adding to aim, the Duellist's to energy, and so on), by rather less a stripe than a rank of the
matching perk. It was built and measured before anything was shown of it. A veteran with every
stripe beat the masters of artillery and of the lancers in a hundred flights of a hundred, where
those fights are set to be won half the time; at half the size it was ninety-eight and a hundred.
And with every perk besides, the duellist's own master went from four wins in five to one in
four: with more energy than any pilot has had, the automatic pilot flies a duellist worse, and
the odds a briefing shows would have fallen for earning a stripe.

Anything added on top of every perk takes a pilot outside what the game has been measured for. A
perk point does not: it is spent on the perks there are.

### Where it is seen

- **The classes page**: three small marks on each class's row, filled as stripes are earned; in
  the panel of the class under the cursor, the contracts won at which its next stripe comes; at
  the foot of the page, either side of the hint, how many stripes are held and how many the next
  point asks for.
- **The perks page**: the points to spend include the stripes', and a line says how many of them
  stripes have brought.
- **The briefing**, on the page of the build the contract will be flown in: the marks of that
  build's class and the contracts won as it of those its next stripe asks; lit, and in other
  words, when this contract is the last of them. It follows the build that is turned to. Nothing
  is said where a win counts for no class (the simulator). This was added while building: the
  count matters most at the moment a build is chosen, and a page three screens away does not
  put it there.
- **A notice** as a stripe is earned: the class and the stripe's number, and that it brought a
  perk point when it did.
- **Two medals**: for a first stripe, and for all twenty-one.
- **The manual**, the guide and the README.

## Approaches considered

For what a stripe is worth: a bonus to the class itself (it deepens one build and punishes
leaving it); a bonus to one skill in everything flown (tried and measured: see above); or perk
points (chosen: the reward for variety is spent on whatever the pilot likes, and stays inside the
measured game).

For how many stripes make a point: three (seven points, three more than there are ranks to buy),
or five (chosen: four points, which with the levels' nineteen is exactly every rank there is).

For how they are earned: by contracts won as the class (chosen: the count is there, shown and
kept already), by a trial of each class (the masters are that), or by rank on contracts (it would
ask the pilot to fly a new class well before it gives anything for flying it at all).

For where they are kept: a list of stripes in the kept game (something more to go wrong between
versions), or read off the record each time (chosen).

## Constraints

- Every new text has its Dutch, inside the room its kind has. No pictures are needed.
- No balance test of what exists changes. Nothing is merged without the owner's word.

## Left to the builder

The exact words; where the marks stand on a row; how the notice is worded; the medals' names.
