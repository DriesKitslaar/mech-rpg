# Stripes

## Why

A build is one of seven classes, and the game counts what a pilot has won as each. Nothing comes
of the count but three medals and, at the very top, the crowns. A pilot who finds one build that
works has no reason to put another together: the hangar holds seven builds and most players will
fly one.

The owner said variety of builds and the classes matter most, next to a feeling of power. The
standing brief is to keep working on my own judgment towards a game that plays like a finished
one. This round makes flying another class pay, in a way that is kept: what a class teaches goes
with the pilot into every machine.

## Goals

1. **Stripes.** A class gives the pilot a stripe at 3, at 8 and at 15 contracts won flying it.
   Three a class, twenty-one in all.
2. **Each class trains one skill, in every machine.** A stripe adds to one of the pilot's skills
   (the things perks already raise) whatever is flown afterwards: the Sharpshooter's stripes to
   aim, the Skirmisher's to evasion, and so on. Trying a class makes every build a little better.
3. **Seen where classes and skills are seen.** The pilot's page of classes shows each class's
   stripes; the page of perks shows what the stripes add to each skill; a stripe is announced as
   it is earned; the briefing's odds count them, because the fight does.
4. An APK after every step.

## Not goals

- No new rule of combat and no new kind of bonus: a stripe adds what a rank of a perk adds, less.
- No stripe for the simulator, a lent machine or the Gauntlet's own frame: they are nobody's class.
- Nothing is taken away: a stripe once earned is kept, through every telling, like a perk.
- The measurements the balance tests hold (the masters, the seat, the jobs) are made with pilots
  who have no stripes, as they were. No balance test of what exists changes.
- No new page and no new option.

## What would make it a failure

- A stripe that makes one class better only at being itself: that would reward flying one build
  for ever, which is the opposite of what this is for.
- Stripes large enough to carry a fight. All twenty-one together add about what ten perk points
  would, where a pilot has nineteen: whoever holds them all has won a hundred and five contracts
  in seven kinds of machine, and is meant to feel it. But the masters and the seat must still be
  fights for that pilot, and that is measured, not assumed.
- A kept game from before this round that loads with stripes it should not have, or without the
  ones its record has earned: the record is what counts, and nothing new is stored.
- A briefing whose odds leave the stripes out, or count them twice.
- A page whose rows or words do not fit, in either language.

## How it works

### Earning them

The pilot's record already says how many contracts were won as each class (the WON column of the
classes page). Stripes are read off it: one at 3, two at 8, three at 15. Nothing new is kept, so a
game from before this round has at once the stripes its record has earned, and is told so once.

### What each class trains

| Class | Skill | A stripe adds | The perk that raises the same |
|---|---|---|---|
| Duellist | Energy every turn | +10 | Capacitor Tuning (+20 a rank) |
| Sharpshooter | Hit chance | +2 | Marksman (+4) |
| Artillery | Ammunition and repair costs | -4% | Quartermaster (-15%) |
| Lancer | Heat shed | +3 | Heat Sinks (+5) |
| Bastion | Armour | +3% | Reinforced Plating (+6%) |
| Skirmisher | Evasion | +2 | Reflexes (+4) |
| All-rounder | Pay | +3% | Haggler (+8%) |

Each is the thing the class lives by: a duellist must boost before every strike, a lancer runs
hot, artillery pays for every salvo, an all-rounder takes any work. What a stripe adds is added
to what the pilot's perks give, in every battle the perks count in and on every bill.

### Where it is seen

- **The classes page**: three small marks on each class's row, filled as stripes are earned; and
  in the panel of the class under the cursor, which skill it trains and how far along it is.
- **The perks page**: under what a perk does, what the stripes of its class have added to it, and
  when the next comes.
- **A notice** as a stripe is earned: the class, the stripe's number, and what it adds.
- **Two medals**: for a first stripe, and for all twenty-one.
- **The manual**, the guide and the README.

## Approaches considered

For what a stripe is worth: a bonus to the class itself (it deepens one build and punishes
leaving it), a bonus to everything flown (chosen: the reward for variety is felt in the build the
pilot likes best), or perk points (the same thing less plainly, and it would make every class
teach the same).

For how they are earned: by contracts won as the class (chosen: the count is there, shown and
kept already), by a trial of each class (the masters are that), or by rank on contracts (it would
ask the pilot to fly a new class well before it gives anything for flying it at all).

For where they are kept: a list of stripes in the kept game (something more to go wrong between
versions), or read off the record each time (chosen).

## Constraints

- Every new text has its Dutch, inside the room its kind has. No pictures are needed.
- No balance test of what exists changes. Nothing is merged without the owner's word.

## Left to the builder

The exact words; where the marks stand on a row; how the notice is worded; the medals' names.
