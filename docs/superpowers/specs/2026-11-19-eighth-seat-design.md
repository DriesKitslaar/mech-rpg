# The Eighth Seat

## Why

The masters gave each class a summit. A pilot who has taken all seven crowns has the medal that
counts them and nothing left to climb: the last thing the arena has to say to its best pilot is a
row of seven words that all read CROWN. The game's two finest frames are won from duels at the end
of the story, long before that; nothing is won from being the best there is.

The owner's own words on what matters were a feeling of power, variety in builds, and machines for
the end of the game that are worth the wait. The standing brief is to keep working, on my own
judgment, towards a game that plays like a finished one. This round gives the arena its last fight
and the game its last machine.

## Goals

1. **The eighth seat.** The masters' page has an eighth row from the start, locked, counting the
   crowns it asks for. With all seven held it opens: one duel against **Touchstone**, who keeps the
   seat, in a machine nobody sells. Any build may fight. The machine fights three ways and changes
   as it is hurt: at range, then with light, then hand to hand.
2. **The Sovereign.** Touchstone's frame: four parts of an original design, with pictures of their
   own from the front and from behind. When the seat is taken the yard that built it takes the new
   holder's order: the parts are in the shop from then on, at the highest prices in it.
3. **What the seat gives.** It is seen: on the masters' page, on the pilot's page, in Touchstone's
   paint, and in a medal. It is said once when it is taken, and a message says the yard is open.
4. An APK after every step.

## Not goals

- No new weapons, generators, modules or rules of combat. The Sovereign is made of what a frame
  part can already be: armour, resistances, aim, a field in its core, arms that keep their balance.
- The seven masters, their crowns and what a crown gives stay as they are.
- No class is asked for: the eighth duel is for whatever the pilot thinks is their best machine.
- No story scene. The seat is the arena's own matter, told on its page and in two messages.

## What would make it a failure

- A seat taken without all seven crowns, or lost, or said to be taken twice; a kept game from
  before this round that does not load, or loads with a seat it did not win.
- A duel no honest late build can win, or one any such build wins without trying; a duel that drags.
- A Sovereign that makes what is left of the game a formality: the back room and the masters must
  still be fights in one.
- The Sovereign on sale to a pilot who has not taken the seat, in any telling.
- A machine that is taken for one out of somebody else's game: the design is the game's own.
- A page whose rows or words do not fit, in either language.

## How it works

### The seat on the page

The masters' page lists the seven classes, and under them a row that is not a class: THE EIGHTH
SEAT. Until the pilot holds every crown it cannot be entered; its note counts the crowns held of
the seven, and the panel under the list says whom the seat's keeper takes a challenge from. With
seven crowns it reads as a master's row does: what the duel pays, and after it is won, that the
seat is the pilot's. The picture beside the list is Touchstone's machine in Touchstone's paint,
from the start: what is to be had is on show.

A opens a briefing like a master's: how Touchstone fights, what they say, the build row and the
odds. No line asks for a class. No wingman comes.

### The duel

One battle in the arena against one machine, with a boss's entrance. It begins as a marksman's
machine does, with weapons that charge; when its core is down to two thirds it changes to energy
weapons and moves more; at one third it throws its guns away for two blades and comes in. Each
change is said in a line of Touchstone's, and each steadies the machine as a boss's change does.

How strong it is is set by measurement, as the masters were: against the late build the game
suggests for each class, flown on Auto Turn by a pilot with a veteran's six ranks of perks, a
build that suits the fight takes the seat about half the time; with no perks seldom; with every
perk usually. The tests hold the measure as ranges.

The duel pays 15000 the first time and a quarter of it after, as the masters' do.

### The Sovereign

A frame built for one pilot: tall, plated like a herald's coat, a crest of seven points on its
head. In figures it stands a little above the Auditor in armour and between the Auditor and the
Sterling in speed; its core raises a small field and its arms keep their balance through a
blade's stroke, as the two duel frames' do. It pays for that in weight, in what it draws from the
generator, and in price. On its own legs it is an all-rounder; what it carries makes it any class.

It is sold only to a pilot who has taken the seat, in every telling: like an arena prize, it is
not something a vendor remembers.

### What the seat gives

- The masters' page and the pilot's page say the seat is held.
- Touchstone's paint, black as the stone with gold in it, is the holder's to wear.
- A medal, of the highest kind.
- A message before (an invitation, when the seventh crown is taken) and a message after (the yard's
  order book), and a word across the screen when the seat is taken.

## Approaches considered

For the last fight: all seven masters back to back (it is the seven fights the pilot has already
won, and it drags); three machines in a row (three smaller fights, and nothing of the Sovereign to
see); or one machine that fights three ways (chosen: one opponent worth the build-up, and the
rules for a machine that changes as it is hurt are there and tried).

For the prize: the frame given outright (a gift with nothing to decide), a single copy that
cannot be bought again (and cannot be tuned twice or kept in two builds), or the right to order it
(chosen: late money has something to go to, and the parts are parts like any other).

For who may enter: a pilot who has beaten all seven masters in anything (it skips what the crowns
are for), or one who holds all seven crowns (chosen).

## Constraints

- Twelve pictures for the frame at the service's lowest quality, inside the budget the ledger
  keeps; nothing else is drawn.
- Every new text has its Dutch, inside the room its kind has. Names stay as they are.
- No balance test of what exists changes. Nothing is merged without the owner's word.

## Left to the builder

The Sovereign's exact figures and prices and Touchstone's weapons, by measurement; the words;
where on the pilot's page the seat is said; the paint's exact colours.
