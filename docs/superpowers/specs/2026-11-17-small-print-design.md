# The Small Print: design

> Let op: dit document verklapt het verborgen verhaal van het spel, tot en met het derde einde.
> This document gives away the hidden story of the game, up to and including the third ending.

## Why

The user asked whether the story is gripping, cryptic and motivating enough "in the way of From's
games", and named the latest Armored Core as the one they loved. The honest answer was: the tone is
right (terse briefings that sometimes lie, an exclusive choice of client, a good turn in the third
act), but there is nothing to work out, the story is silent while a battle is fought, and playing
it again shows nothing that was hidden.

What is taken from those games is a way of telling, not anything told: a truth under the story
that nobody states; found documents and objects that each say only what their writer would say;
voices during the fighting; a second telling in which what was learned can be acted on; an ending
that only that second telling can reach. Every name, person, place, machine and event here is this
game's own.

## Goals

- A hidden layer under the story as it stands, which makes its existing lines mean more than they
  seemed to. No existing line is contradicted.
- Things to find (papers and brass lamp checks), earned by what the pilot does, kept through the
  cycles, shown with their gaps.
- People who speak during a battle about more than tactics.
- A second cycle that is not the first one again: the frame's recorder speaks where it has been
  before, three contracts and a conversation exist only then, and a third verdict can be reached.
- A third ending with its own last contract, its own machine, its own place and its own pages.

## Not goals

- No change to the rules of a battle beyond two new moments at which a line can be said.
- No change to what a saved game holds (finds are kept in the list the database's entries are kept in).
- No new mode, no new currency, no map.

## What would make it a failure

- A find, a line or a page that explains the hidden layer in plain words.
- A third ending that a pilot can stumble into without having understood anything, or one that
  nobody can reach without a guide.
- Anything that is another game's: a name, a person, a plot, a line.

## The truth under the story

Nothing in the game says any of this outright. It is written down here so that everything that
hints at it agrees.

1. **The Society.** When the first shaft of Varn was sunk (the Meridian shaft, "No. 1"), its
   diggers founded the Meridian Pit Mutual Aid Society. Rule 1: a tenth of every wage, "in iron",
   to the box. The box buried the dead and fed the hurt. Rule 9: nine checks on the board make a
   meeting. A check is a brass lamp check, one to a member, handed down in families. The first
   policy the Society wrote, VRN-1, covered the pit village against war, riot and civil disturbance.
2. **The dry year, forty years ago.** The seam ran out. Hadron's board kept the surveyor's report
   in the room, and to get at the box (forty years of tenths) found the pumps of the bottom level
   "uneconomic". The Society's hall, its members' streets and its box went under water. The last
   meeting in the hall was adjourned for want of a quorum; "the Secretary will act for the Society
   until one is had". The Secretary has acted for forty years. Run by its Secretary alone, the
   Society became Meridian Mutual: an insurer owned by members who can never meet. Hadron and
   Lumen were joined to VRN-1 later.
3. **What Varn is now.** Hadron mines nothing: its ore trains run empty so that the books stay
   alive, and the city lives on Meridian's credit. Lumen counts the heads below the gates every
   night and forecasts the year after which no meeting can be had. The floodgates are bolted from
   above: they keep the people of the bottom levels (the Undertow, the members' children and
   grandchildren) in, and uncounted. The Secretary holds a proxy for every member who drowned.
4. **The convoy, eleven years ago.** In a dry year the hall could be reached. The Undertow brought
   the box up and sent it, with members, in four trucks by the sluice, to be opened at the board
   office. Escort: Old Sixty in the lead, and a second pilot in the frame that is now the pilot's.
   Meridian's Desk 9 bought a new licence, aged twenty, to "stop a freight convoy at the sluice"
   without opening the trucks: Ashfall. Sixty was not where he should have been. The second escort
   stayed, saw the box go into the water at gate four, marked the place on the frame's plate, and
   got the blame: Sixty paid a clerk to file the loss under the second escort's callsign. The
   licence was cancelled, the frame impounded, and the loss booked against the frame as a debt.
   The second escort took the only work going, the night desk. The second escort is Dispatch.
5. **The pilot.** Two years ago Dispatch sold a bundle of pilots' debts to keep the desk open, and
   Desk 9 bought it, because the frame's debt was in it. When the frame came up as salvage Dispatch
   issued it, debt and all, to a new licence: the pilot. Its recorder was never wiped.
6. **The war.** Desk 9 insured all three sides, sent the squatters of Seam Nine a tank and billed
   Hadron for it as stolen, bought the second booking of one escort slot, and kept Ashfall on a
   retainer with no payer's name for eleven years. The total loss lets the Secretary take the whole
   city "for its members". Schedule C lists the pilot's frame first, because its recorder knows
   where the box is.
7. **The third verdict.** The box has nine hasps: nine checks hung on its lid open it. With the
   roll that is inside and nine checks on the board in the hall (the registered office, which has
   never moved), a meeting is good and the Secretary's proxies fall. The pilot stands in the door
   while the names are read, then breaks the Proxy: the machine that casts the votes of the drowned.
   The motion that the Society resume its business is carried. It is not a clean ending: the
   members now own an insurer with liens on eleven other cities.

### Lines that already carried it

These were written before there was a hidden layer and are why it fits: "a seam that should have
run dry forty years ago"; "a debt nobody has called in yet"; "the association known as the
Undertow" holding a share of the policy; "none of them read it"; Meridian *Mutual*; Seam Nine,
Insurance Desk 9 on the open board, channel nine; "names get people killed down here"; Tamsin
counting heads twice; "nobody below is being counted"; the sixty-first; the clerk who "always
liked a tidy desk"; Dispatch who "never gave a name" and routed every good contract to the pilot;
Krell who "was here before the company"; Leviathan out of "the old drowned yards"; the Gauntlet,
whose song is called The Way Down.

## How it is told

**Voices in a battle.** A line can be said when the battle opens, when a round begins, when it is
won (as before), and now also when one enemy is left of several (`last`) and when the pilot's core
is below half (`hurt`). A line can be kept for a later cycle. Every story contract gets one or two
lines that are about the story rather than about tactics. The frame's recorder (who: `Recorder`,
its own small face) plays back, in a later cycle, what it heard eleven years ago in the places the
convoy passed: the sluice.

**Finds.** A new page of the database, FOUND, after WORLD. Forty finds: twenty-four papers,
numbered in the order of what they are about (the founding first, the ending last), and sixteen
lamp checks. A find not yet found has its row and its number, and its page shows a clue instead of
its text: a few words about where it is, in the game's own voice. A find has a title, a source
(who wrote it, or where it lay), and a text of at most twelve lines. Finds are kept through the
cycles. A toast says when one is found. Under the list: how many are found, and how many checks
are held.

**The tally.** Nine checks make a meeting. There are sixteen to be had, some of them only by one
answer to a question of the story or on one client's road, so nobody can hold all of them in one
cycle, and a first cycle played without side work ends with three or four. Checks are finds, so
they are kept through the cycles.

**A later cycle.** From the second cycle on: a page before the first contract (PLAYBACK); the
recorder's lines; three contracts with no client (`quest-1` to `quest-3`), one at a time: a relay
in the freight lanes that listens for the frame, guarded by machines in a livery nobody should
know yet; gate four at low water, if the frame's logs were not sold; the hall, once the war is
over. After the second, a conversation in which the pilot plays the recorder to Dispatch.

**The third verdict.** After the audit, a pilot who has brought up the box and holds nine checks
is made a third offer, by Tamsin: Any Other Business. Two battles in the drowned hall: the door,
held for six rounds while the checks are hung up and counted; then the Proxy. Three new pages of
epilogue, one for each client the war was fought for.

**After a first ending.** A letter from "A Tidy Desk": the frame has a recorder that was never
asked anything; fly the war again and let it listen.

## Approaches considered

1. **Finds only** (lore pages and nothing to do with them). Cheapest; but what is understood can
   never be acted on, which is what makes the telling worth the trouble.
2. **A fourth act for everybody, in the first cycle.** Simple to reach; but then nothing is hidden,
   and the second cycle stays the first one again.
3. **Chosen: a layer that a first cycle shows and a later cycle lets the pilot act on.** The first
   cycle gives what Meridian is and that nine checks make a meeting; only a later cycle gives where
   the box is and who the second escort was, and offers the third verdict. It costs three contracts,
   a conversation and an ending, and it makes the six questions the story already asks matter more.

## Constraints

- A saved game from before loads, and finds whose time has come are found at once, in one toast.
- Every new English text has its Dutch, inside the room its kind has.
- Image spend: 5.18 of 14 dollars were spent before this round. This round may spend up to 1 dollar,
  at low quality: a face for the recorder, the hall, a still for the third ending, the Proxy.
- Nothing is merged without the user's word.

## Left to the builder

The exact words (they are in the content files), where on the page a find's source goes, the sound
of a find, the Proxy's numbers (set by simulation, like every boss's).
